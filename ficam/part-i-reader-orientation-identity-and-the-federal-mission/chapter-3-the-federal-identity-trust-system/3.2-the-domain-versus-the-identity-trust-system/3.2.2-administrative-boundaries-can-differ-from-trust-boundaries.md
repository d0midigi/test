# 3.2.2 Administrative Boundaries Can Differ From Trust Boundaries

Federal environments make this distinction even more pronounced because administrative responsibility may be distributed across multiple commands, agencies, enterprise service providers, contractors, and mission partners.

A local command may administer an application but depend upon an enterprise PKI service.

A component may administer its own Active Directory forest while consuming identities originating from a broader department-level source.

A contractor may operate infrastructure under government authority while relying on enterprise authentication services maintained elsewhere.

A mission partner may authenticate users through an identity provider outside the local administrative boundary.

Administrative ownership therefore does not necessarily indicate where trust originates.

A system can be locally managed while relying upon remotely administered identity authority.

That creates a shared security dependency.

If the upstream identity authority is compromised, the downstream system may accept malicious identity information even though its own administrators have made no local configuration change.

Conversely, an upstream identity authority may remain secure while a local relying system interprets valid identity data incorrectly or grants excessive authorization.

Both sides of the relationship matter.
