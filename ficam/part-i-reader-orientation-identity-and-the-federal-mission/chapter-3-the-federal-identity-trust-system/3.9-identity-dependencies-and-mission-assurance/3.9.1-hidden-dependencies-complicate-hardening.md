# 3.9.1 Hidden Dependencies Complicate Hardening

Identity hardening often exposes dependencies that routine operation concealed.

Disabling NTLM may reveal an application that never migrated to Kerberos.

Restricting privileged logon may expose an administrative workflow built around direct access from ordinary workstations.

Rotating a service-account password may reveal undocumented services using the same credential.

Requiring stronger certificate mapping may expose assumptions in older authentication implementations.

Removing SIDHistory may break access still dependent on migration-era identifiers.

These are not arguments against hardening.

They are evidence that the identity architecture contains undocumented coupling.

A mature hardening program treats those failures as discovery rather than as justification for abandoning the target state.

The dependency is mapped, assigned an owner, evaluated for mission impact, and either removed or governed as an explicit exception.

Hardening then becomes an iterative engineering process rather than a one-time configuration exercise.
