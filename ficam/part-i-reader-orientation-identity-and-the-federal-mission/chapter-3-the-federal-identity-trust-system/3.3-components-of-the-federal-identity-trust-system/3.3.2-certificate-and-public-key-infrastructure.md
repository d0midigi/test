# 3.3.2 Certificate and Public Key Infrastructure

Public Key Infrastructure adds cryptographically backed credentials to the trust system.

In federal environments, this includes the broader PKI relationships supporting PIV and CAC credentials as well as locally or enterprise-operated certificate services used for servers, devices, applications, users, and authentication.

Certificate trust changes the identity model because possession of the correct private key and a trusted certificate can establish identity without presenting the represented account's password.

This places security authority in multiple locations simultaneously: the Certification Authority that issues the certificate, the template or policy governing issuance, the process used to validate the requester, the private key protecting the credential, the revocation infrastructure determining whether the credential remains acceptable, and the mapping process associating the certificate with an account or identity.

A certificate consumer ultimately sees a credential that validates.

The security of that decision depends on everything upstream.
