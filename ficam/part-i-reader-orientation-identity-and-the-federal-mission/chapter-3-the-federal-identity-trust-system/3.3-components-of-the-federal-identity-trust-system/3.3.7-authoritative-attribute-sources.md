# 3.3.7 Authoritative Attribute Sources

Identity systems make decisions using more than usernames and credentials.

Attributes matter.

Employment status, agency affiliation, organizational assignment, clearance-related information where applicable to the access model, sponsorship, role, device status, contract status, mission assignment, and other authoritative characteristics may influence provisioning or access.

Those attributes often originate outside Active Directory.

Human-resources systems, personnel systems, contract-management systems, authoritative registries, and other enterprise sources may feed identity lifecycle processes.

This creates another form of upstream trust.

If an identity-governance system provisions access based on authoritative data, the integrity of that source affects downstream access decisions.

An incorrectly changed attribute may cause an identity to receive entitlements it should not possess even though authentication itself remains completely valid.

The security of authorization therefore sometimes begins before the account reaches Active Directory.

Identity governance inherits the integrity of the data from which identity is constructed.

That is why authoritative attribute sources belong inside the federal identity trust model rather than being treated merely as business-system inputs.
