# 3.8.2 Shared Services Concentrate Both Value and Dependency

Federal enterprises increasingly rely on shared services because duplication is expensive and difficult to manage.

Shared PKI, authentication, cloud identity, network services, monitoring, vulnerability management, and administrative platforms can provide substantial consistency and efficiency.

They also concentrate dependency.

A locally operated identity service can fail locally.

An enterprise service can fail broadly.

The same principle applies to compromise.

A shared service trusted by many components may provide an adversary with a larger downstream influence than a service restricted to one enclave.

This does not argue against shared services.

It argues for protecting them according to the aggregate trust they carry.

The larger the downstream dependency, the stronger the requirements should become for administrative isolation, change control, monitoring, recovery, and independent validation.

Centralization increases efficiency by reducing duplication.

Security engineering must ensure it does not also centralize unbounded failure.
