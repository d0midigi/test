# 3.8.1 Administrative Authority Can Cross Contractual Boundaries

Federal systems are often operated through combinations of government personnel, prime contractors, subcontractors, enterprise service providers, and externally managed platforms.

Those organizational distinctions matter legally and contractually.

They do not necessarily constrain technical reach.

A contractor-operated endpoint-management platform may deploy software to government systems.

A vendor-supported appliance may authenticate administrators through government Active Directory.

A managed cloud service may rely on federated government identity.

An externally operated security platform may ingest highly sensitive identity telemetry.

These relationships should be treated as control paths.

The environment must understand which external parties can administer systems, which identities they use, where those identities originate, how privilege is approved, what systems accept it, and how quickly that authority can be removed when the contractual or operational relationship changes.

The word contractor should never function as a substitute for architectural analysis.
