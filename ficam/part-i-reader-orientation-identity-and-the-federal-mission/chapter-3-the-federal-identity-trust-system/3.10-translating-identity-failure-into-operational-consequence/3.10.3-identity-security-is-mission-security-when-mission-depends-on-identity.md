# 3.10.3 Identity Security Is Mission Security When Mission Depends on Identity

The phrase should not be used as a slogan.

It is a dependency statement.

Where a mission capability depends on identity to authenticate users, assign authority, administer systems, issue credentials, enforce access, or recover trusted state, compromise of the identity system can become compromise of the mission capability.

The degree of impact depends on the relationship.

That relationship is what the rest of _Contested Terrain_ will continue to expose.

Federal Identity, Credential, and Access Management provides a framework for establishing and governing trustworthy identity. Active Directory and the surrounding identity infrastructure implement significant portions of that trust in real systems. Offensive operations reveal where those implementations can be subverted. Defensive engineering attempts to constrain the resulting authority. Detection identifies when trusted mechanisms begin behaving inconsistently with their intended purpose. Recovery establishes whether the environment can again rely on the identities it accepts.

Those disciplines converge on the same operational requirement:

The mission must be able to distinguish authorized authority from adversary-controlled authority.

That requirement is why the identity trust system deserves to be treated as infrastructure in its own right.
