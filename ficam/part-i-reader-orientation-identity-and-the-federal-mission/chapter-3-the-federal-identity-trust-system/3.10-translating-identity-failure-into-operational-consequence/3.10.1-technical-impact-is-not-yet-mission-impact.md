# 3.10.1 Technical Impact Is Not Yet Mission Impact

Technical impact describes what an adversary can do to the technology.

Mission impact describes what that technical capability allows the adversary to affect operationally.

Compromise of an application administrator may permit application configuration changes.

If the application supports an administrative convenience function, the mission consequence may be limited.

If the same application supports operational planning, communications, logistics, intelligence support, or another mission-essential function, the significance changes.

The identity-security practitioner should not invent mission impact merely because the system is important.

The connection must be defensible.

This usually requires understanding what the system supports, how the compromised identity influences the system, whether the attacker can affect confidentiality, integrity, availability, or administrative control, and what operational dependency exists downstream.

The stronger the technical chain, the stronger the resulting risk statement.
