# 3.10.2 Loss of Trust Can Exceed Loss of Availability

Some of the most serious identity incidents do not initially make systems unavailable.

They make identity state unreliable.

If an attacker acquires sufficient authority to alter privileged group membership, issue trusted authentication certificates, forge Kerberos tickets, modify federation assertions, or manipulate authoritative identity data, defenders may no longer be able to accept existing identity state at face value.

This creates a different operational condition from an outage.

An outage is visible and bounded by the services that are unavailable.

Loss of trust can remain hidden while affected systems continue functioning.

The resulting recovery burden may also be greater. Systems must not merely be restarted; authoritative identity state must be reconstructed and revalidated.

This distinction becomes particularly important for mission leadership because service availability can create a false sense of security.

The enterprise may still be running.

Its decisions about who is authorized may no longer be trustworthy.
