# 3.5.3 JWICS and National Security Systems (NSS)

Joint Worldwide Intelligence Communications System (JWICS) and other National Security Systems operate under security requirements appropriate to highly sensitive missions and information.

The identity-security challenge in such environments is not simply to reproduce commercial enterprise controls at a higher classification.

Operational requirements may produce very different architecture.

Connectivity may be tightly restricted. Systems may depend upon specialized applications. Administrative activity may occur through dedicated workstations and management networks. External services commonly assumed to be available in commercial identity architectures may be absent or intentionally unreachable.

That changes how identity controls must be implemented.

A design that assumes constant access to an external identity provider may be unsuitable. A recovery process that assumes easy retrieval of cloud-hosted documentation may fail. Certificate validation, directory replication, software deployment, and logging may all operate under tighter connectivity restrictions.

Security architecture must therefore reflect the actual operating environment rather than importing assumptions from a continuously connected commercial network.

The principle becomes especially important in recovery.

A highly secured environment that cannot restore identity services under degraded conditions has exchanged one form of risk for another.
