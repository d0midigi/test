# 3.5.2 SIPRNet

The Secret Internet Protocol Router Network (SIPRNet) supports classified information and systems at the Secret level within authorized environments.

Its classification boundary changes the operational consequences of identity compromise but does not remove the fundamental identity-security problems found elsewhere.

Users must still authenticate.

Systems must still determine authorization.

Administrative identities must still be separated from ordinary users.

Service accounts and machine identities still exist.

Credentials can still be mishandled.

Group membership can still become excessive.

Delegation and management dependencies can still produce indirect authority.

The security controls surrounding the enclave may be substantially stronger than those protecting ordinary enterprise networks, yet the internal identity system still contains relationships that must be engineered correctly.

This is an important corrective to an assumption that appears occasionally in highly controlled environments: a strong external boundary does not compensate for weak internal identity relationships.

An attacker who obtains a valid foothold inside the boundary may benefit precisely because defenders assumed that crossing the boundary was the difficult part.

The stronger the perimeter, the more damaging excessive implicit trust inside it can become.
