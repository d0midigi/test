# 3.5 Federal and Military Enclaves

Federal identity infrastructure rarely exists inside a single homogeneous network.

Large departments and defense environments may operate multiple enclaves separated by mission, classification, sensitivity, operational purpose, geographic location, administrative authority, or connectivity constraints. Those enclaves may have different identity architectures even when they ultimately support the same workforce.

This creates an important distinction between a person's real-world identity and the digital identities through which that person operates.

One person may possess several accounts because the systems in which those accounts exist cannot simply share one directory. The accounts may use related identifying attributes, derive from the same authoritative person record, or be associated with the same federal credential while remaining technically distinct identities within separate security domains.

From an operational perspective, this can appear redundant.

From a security perspective, the separation may be deliberate.

A compromise in one enclave should not automatically provide equivalent authority in another.

The challenge is maintaining enough identity coherence to support mission operations without accidentally converting interoperability into uncontrolled transitive trust.
