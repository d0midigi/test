# 3.5.1 NIPRNet

The Non-classified Internet Protocol Router Network (NIPRNet) supports unclassified Department of Defense networking and provides connectivity for a wide range of administrative, operational-support, collaboration, and enterprise services.

Identity within NIPRNet environments is frequently broad because the network supports large populations of users, endpoints, applications, enterprise services, and external connectivity requirements.

That breadth makes identity segmentation especially important.

The mere fact that two systems exist within an unclassified DoD environment does not mean that the same administrative identities should control both or that authentication between them should be unrestricted. Enterprise services, local command infrastructure, contractor systems, development environments, security platforms, and mission applications may occupy significantly different trust positions despite sharing the same broad network classification.

An attacker who establishes a foothold on NIPRNet is therefore not automatically in possession of meaningful mission authority.

The danger emerges when directory relationships, credential reuse, administrative tooling, management infrastructure, or trust connections allow the initial access to expand.

In this sense, NIPRNet illustrates the distinction established earlier in this book: network presence is not equivalent to identity authority.

The attacker may be inside the network while still needing to solve the harder problem of reaching trusted identity.
