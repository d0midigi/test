# 3.6 Cross-Domain Solutions and Identity

Cross-domain environments introduce a security condition that ordinary enterprise federation does not fully capture.

The systems being connected may operate under different classification levels, security policies, releasability rules, or information-protection requirements. The boundary between them exists specifically because unrestricted movement across that boundary would be unacceptable.

Cross-Domain Solutions (CDSs) therefore do not simply provide connectivity between networks.

They enforce carefully controlled interaction between security domains.

Identity becomes relevant because many cross-domain decisions ultimately depend upon who is requesting an action, what attributes are associated with that identity, what system the request originates from, what information is being accessed, and whether policy permits the requested movement or use.

The identity context may therefore influence a decision that networking alone cannot make.

A packet can be technically routable while still being unauthorized to cross the security boundary.
