# 3.6.1 Cross-Domain Identity Is Not Ordinary Domain Trust

It would be dangerous to think of cross-domain identity simply as another variation of an Active Directory trust.

An Active Directory trust primarily establishes authentication relationships between directory security domains.

A cross-domain security boundary addresses a larger policy problem.

The participating environments may intentionally prevent broad authentication trust from crossing the boundary. Information movement may be mediated through specialized guards, transfer mechanisms, access solutions, or applications operating under highly constrained policy.

Identity may be carried across that architecture, but it must not silently erase the boundary the Cross-Domain Solution exists to preserve.

This creates a different security goal from ordinary enterprise interoperability.

The objective is controlled recognition, not seamless trust.
