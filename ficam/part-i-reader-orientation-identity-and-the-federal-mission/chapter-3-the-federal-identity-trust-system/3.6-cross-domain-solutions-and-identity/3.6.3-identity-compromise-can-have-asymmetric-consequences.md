# 3.6.3 Identity Compromise Can Have Asymmetric Consequences

A compromise originating in a lower-sensitivity environment does not automatically imply compromise of a higher-sensitivity environment.

Well-designed cross-domain architecture exists partly to prevent exactly that outcome.

But identity relationships can create asymmetric risks when information or credentials accepted on one side influence decisions on the other.

If the higher-side environment accepts identity assertions, attributes, certificates, or authentication state originating elsewhere, the integrity of those upstream mechanisms matters.

The correct defensive objective is not to assume that lower-side identity can never be trusted.

It is to ensure that the form and extent of that trust are explicit, constrained, validated, and monitored.

Cross-domain identity should therefore be analyzed as a deliberate exception to separation, not as proof that the separation has disappeared.
