# 3.6.2 Attribute Integrity Becomes Critical

Cross-domain decisions may depend heavily on attributes.

Identity alone may not be enough.

The system may need to know affiliation, mission assignment, role, releasability context, clearance-related authorization as implemented by the applicable architecture, device state, or another attribute relevant to the policy decision.

When access depends on those attributes, their integrity becomes security-critical.

An attacker does not necessarily need to impersonate another person if they can manipulate the attribute that causes the access decision to change.

This is a recurring theme across FICAM architectures more generally: authorization can be subverted through identity data even when the authentication event remains valid.

The user is still the user.

The system has been given the wrong facts about what that user should be permitted to do.

Cross-domain environments make that distinction particularly consequential because the resulting decision may determine whether information or capability is permitted to move across a highly sensitive boundary.
