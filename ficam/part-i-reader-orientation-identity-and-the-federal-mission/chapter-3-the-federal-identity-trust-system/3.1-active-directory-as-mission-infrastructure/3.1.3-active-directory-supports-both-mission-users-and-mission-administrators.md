# 3.1.3 Active Directory Supports Both Mission Users and Mission Administrators

Identity infrastructure affects two substantially different categories of access: use of a mission capability and administration of the systems providing that capability.

The distinction is important because compromise of administrative identity usually creates greater strategic consequence.

A normal user may be authorized to consume a service.

An administrator may be authorized to change the service itself.

Active Directory frequently participates in both.

A domain group may determine who can use an application while another group determines who can administer its operating system. A third group may control the virtualization platform hosting it. Another identity may administer the backup infrastructure capable of restoring it. Yet another may manage the network controls governing connectivity.

From a mission perspective, all of those identities may ultimately influence the same service.

This is why the privileged-access discussion later in the book extends beyond traditional Domain Admin accounts. Mission systems are governed through multiple administrative planes, and any identity capable of exercising control over those planes may affect the trustworthiness of the mission capability.

The directory provides a common thread through many of them.
