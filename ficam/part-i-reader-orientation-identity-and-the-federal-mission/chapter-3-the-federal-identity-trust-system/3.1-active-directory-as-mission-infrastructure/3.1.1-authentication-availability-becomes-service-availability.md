# 3.1.1 Authentication Availability Becomes Service Availability

When multiple enterprise services depend upon a common authentication infrastructure, authentication availability becomes a shared operational dependency.

An individual application may be fully functional at the software and database layers but effectively unavailable if users cannot establish identities that the application accepts.

A server may be healthy but inaccessible because authorization data cannot be obtained or interpreted correctly.

A remote-access service may establish a network connection successfully while users remain unable to reach mission resources because downstream identity services are unavailable.

This coupling can make identity infrastructure a multiplier of operational impact.

The failure of one application affects one application.

The failure of an identity service may affect many applications simultaneously.

That does not mean every Active Directory outage produces enterprise-wide failure. Cached logon behavior, service-local authorization, application design, replicated Domain Controllers, redundant network paths, and other resilience mechanisms can reduce impact. The important point is that dependency exists and should be understood before an outage or compromise forces the issue.

Mission assurance therefore requires more than maintaining Domain Controller uptime statistics.

It requires understanding what mission services depend upon the identity plane, how those dependencies fail, and which capabilities remain usable when portions of the identity system are degraded.
