# 3.1.2 Directory Integrity Is Mission-Relevant Integrity

Availability is only one part of the problem.

A functioning directory whose authoritative state has been maliciously altered may present a greater risk than a directory that is simply offline.

Suppose an adversary modifies membership in a group that controls a mission application. The application itself may remain completely healthy. Its database may be intact. Its operating system may be fully patched. Its network connections may be functioning normally.

Its authorization decision is nevertheless wrong because the identity data on which that decision depends has been corrupted.

The application has inherited the directory's integrity failure.

The same principle applies to Group Policy, service accounts, computer objects, delegation, trust configuration, certificate mappings, and other identity relationships. Downstream systems frequently assume that directory information represents authorized administrative state.

When that assumption becomes false, systems can continue operating while enforcing adversary-controlled policy.

This is one reason identity compromise can remain difficult to recognize. The attacker may not need to make a service fail.

They may only need to make the service trust the wrong principal.
