# 3.7.2 Partner Access Must Have a Lifecycle

External identities often become security problems not when they are first created, but when the reason for their access disappears.

A mission ends.

A contractor changes roles.

A coalition participant rotates.

An interagency project concludes.

A sponsored account outlives its sponsor.

If external access does not possess a reliable lifecycle, the environment accumulates identities whose continued legitimacy becomes increasingly difficult to establish.

This is especially dangerous because partner identities may not be visible through ordinary local personnel processes.

A local employee who separates may trigger familiar offboarding mechanisms.

A mission-partner identity may depend on federation state, sponsorship, application-specific authorization, guest-account lifecycle, or another mechanism owned by a different team.

The solution is not necessarily to shorten every access period arbitrarily.

It is to ensure that external access possesses an explicit reason for existence, an authoritative owner, an expiration or recertification mechanism where appropriate, and a reliable means of revocation.

Trust should be renewable.

It should not be immortal.
