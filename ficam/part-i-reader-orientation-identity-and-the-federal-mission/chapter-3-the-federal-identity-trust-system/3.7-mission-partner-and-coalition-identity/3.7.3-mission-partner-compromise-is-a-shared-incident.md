# 3.7.3 Mission-Partner Compromise Is a Shared Incident

Federated or partner identity changes the incident-response model.

If a local account is compromised, the local organization can usually disable the account, reset credentials, terminate sessions, and investigate the surrounding activity directly.

If the malicious access originates through an external identity provider, local defenders may control only part of the response.

They can terminate local sessions.

They can remove local authorization.

They can restrict or suspend the trust relationship.

They may not be able to revoke the originating credential.

That responsibility belongs to the external authority.

This creates an operational requirement for coordination that is every bit as important as the federation configuration itself.

An architecture that can establish partner trust in seconds but has no tested mechanism for jointly revoking that trust during an incident is incomplete.

Security design must therefore include the failure mode.

How is a compromised external identity reported?

Who can disable access locally?

Who contacts the identity-owning organization?

Can one partner identity be blocked without terminating the entire federation relationship?

Can a compromised issuer or signing authority be isolated?

How quickly can updated trust information propagate?

These questions are not administrative details added after federation.

They are part of the security architecture of federation.
