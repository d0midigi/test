# 2.4.3 Administrative Convenience Produces Security Reachability

Identity attack surface tends to grow alongside administrative convenience.

Centralized management is valuable because it allows a relatively small number of administrators and systems to manage a large enterprise efficiently. The same centralization necessarily creates pathways through which administrative authority reaches many targets.

A software-deployment platform requires authority to install software.

A backup platform requires authority to read and restore protected data.

A virtualization platform requires authority over guest systems.

An endpoint-management platform requires authority over managed devices.

An identity-governance platform requires authority to create, modify, and disable accounts.

A privileged-access system may broker access to highly sensitive assets.

These systems exist because decentralized manual administration would be inefficient or impossible at enterprise scale.

Yet their reach changes the security problem.

If an attacker compromises a management platform that can deploy software to privileged systems, the attacker's effective reach may be determined by the management platform rather than the permissions of the initially compromised user.

If a backup administrator can restore or manipulate Domain Controller data, backup administration has identity-security significance.

If a virtualization administrator can access the storage or runtime environment of Domain Controllers, hypervisor administration becomes part of the trusted identity boundary.

The attack surface therefore includes systems whose primary business purpose is not identity at all.

What matters is whether they can control identity infrastructure.
