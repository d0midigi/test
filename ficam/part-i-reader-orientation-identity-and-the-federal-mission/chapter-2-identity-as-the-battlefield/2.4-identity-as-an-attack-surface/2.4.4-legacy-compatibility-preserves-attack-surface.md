# 2.4.4 Legacy Compatibility Preserves Attack Surface

Identity systems carry history.

A new authentication method does not necessarily remove the old one. A new forest does not automatically eliminate old trusts. A migration does not guarantee removal of SIDHistory. A managed service account does not cause traditional service accounts to disappear. Cloud identity does not automatically replace on-premises authentication.

The result is coexistence.

Modern controls frequently sit beside legacy mechanisms because the enterprise cannot remove every dependency simultaneously.

This coexistence creates asymmetric value for an attacker.

The organization may use its newest authentication methods for most users while one legacy application preserves an older protocol that offers a weaker path.

Most administrators may use hardened workstations while one emergency process still requires interactive logon from a less controlled system.

Most service accounts may be managed correctly while one forgotten account retains an old password and broad privilege.

The adversary needs only one usable path.

This is why identity debt becomes operationally significant rather than merely architectural.

Security posture is constrained by the weakest remaining trust path that still reaches something valuable.
