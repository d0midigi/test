# 2.3.5 Certificate and Federation Infrastructure Extend the Control Plane

The identity control plane also extends beyond passwords and Kerberos.

Certificate-based authentication introduces another source of identity authority. When an application or Domain Controller accepts a certificate as proof of identity, trust moves toward the infrastructure that issued, mapped, and validated that certificate.

A Certification Authority capable of issuing trusted authentication certificates therefore participates in the identity control plane even though it is not a Domain Controller.

The same logic applies to federation.

A relying application may accept a token because it trusts the identity provider that signed it. In that relationship, the federation service becomes authoritative for the claims carried within the token.

Hybrid identity introduces another extension. Synchronization systems move selected identity information between on-premises Active Directory and cloud identity environments. Depending on the architecture, a synchronization platform may possess significant permissions in both environments. The system that appears to perform an administrative plumbing function may therefore connect two otherwise distinct control planes.

These examples are important because they expose the limits of treating Active Directory security as protection of Domain Controllers alone.

Domain Controllers remain critical.

They are not the entire trusted core.

Any infrastructure capable of creating, modifying, certifying, synchronizing, federating, or administering trusted identity can become strategically significant.
