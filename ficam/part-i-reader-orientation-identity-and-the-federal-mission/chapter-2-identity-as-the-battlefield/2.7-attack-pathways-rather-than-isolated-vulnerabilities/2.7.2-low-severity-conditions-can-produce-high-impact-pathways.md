# 2.7.2 Low-Severity Conditions Can Produce High-Impact Paths

The severity of an identity weakness cannot always be determined from the weakness in isolation.

Suppose an ordinary user can modify the description attribute of another account. In most environments, that may have little security significance.

Suppose instead the user can modify `msDS-KeyCredentialLink` on a privileged account.

The fact that both actions involve modifying a directory attribute does not make their consequences equivalent.

Likewise, control over a group means little without understanding what the group controls.

Local administrator rights on an isolated workstation differ dramatically from local administrator rights on a server where Tier 0 administrators routinely log on.

A stale service account with a weak password may appear moderately concerning until its group membership is examined.

A certificate template with permissive enrollment may appear benign until the authentication properties and subject-name controls are considered.

Context changes severity.

Attack paths provide that context.

This also explains why remediation based solely on finding count can be misleading. Closing twenty low-impact findings may reduce the number displayed on a dashboard while leaving one viable path to Tier 0 untouched.

Identity-security prioritization should consider what authority the weakness makes reachable.
