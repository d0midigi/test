# 2.7.3 Attack Paths Often Cross Technology Boundaries

The most consequential attack paths are not always contained within Active Directory Domain Services.

An adversary may begin with a directory account, gain local administrator rights on a management server, compromise a backup platform, and use that control to manipulate Domain Controller recovery data.

Another path may begin with Active Directory permissions, move into certificate infrastructure, produce an authentication certificate, and return to Kerberos through Public Key Cryptography for Initial Authentication in Kerberos (PKINIT).

A hybrid path may begin on premises, reach a synchronization system, acquire cloud identity authority, and terminate in a Microsoft Entra administrative role.

The reverse direction is also possible where cloud-controlled identities, applications, synchronization mechanisms, or management systems possess on-premises authority.

These paths reveal why product-centered security reviews can miss systemic risk.

The Active Directory team may correctly secure directory permissions while the virtualization team retains a control path around them.

The certificate team may correctly protect the CA server while unsafe template governance creates an authentication path.

The cloud team may protect privileged Entra roles while a synchronization dependency introduces authority from an on-premises system that receives less protection.

Attack paths cross the organizational boundaries used to administer technology because adversaries are not constrained by the enterprise's org chart.
