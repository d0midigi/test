# 2.5.4 Certificates Move Trust Into Cryptographic Infrastructure

Certificate authentication produces a similar shift.

A system accepting a certificate must determine whether the certificate chains to an accepted authority, remains valid, satisfies the intended usage, has not been revoked under the applicable validation process, and maps appropriately to the identity being represented.

The resulting authentication decision may depend on infrastructure far removed from the application receiving the connection.

That infrastructure includes Certification Authorities, private keys, certificate templates, enrollment permissions, revocation services, trust stores, and mapping logic.

A certificate can therefore be perfectly valid cryptographically and still represent a security failure if it was issued under conditions that allowed the wrong principal to obtain it.

This illustrates a recurring theme in identity security:

Trust can be technically valid while operationally wrong.

The signature verifies.

The certificate chains.

The token validates.

The ticket decrypts.

The directory permission authorizes the operation.

The security failure exists because an adversary acquired control over a mechanism the environment was designed to trust.

That is the nature of contested identity terrain.
