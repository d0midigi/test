# 2.5.1 Trust Can Cross Boundaries Without Removing Them

A trust relationship does not necessarily eliminate a security boundary.

This distinction is especially important in federal environments.

Two agencies may retain separate administrative authority while establishing federation that permits selected users from one agency to access resources in another.

Two Active Directory forests may remain distinct security environments while a forest trust allows carefully controlled authentication across them.

A mission partner may retain control over its own identities while another environment accepts selected assertions or credentials originating from that partner.

The boundary still exists.

Trust creates a controlled crossing point.

The security question therefore becomes whether the crossing remains constrained to its intended purpose.

An Active Directory forest trust, for example, does not automatically give every principal in one forest unrestricted access to the other. Resource permissions, trust direction, authentication scope, SID filtering, selective authentication, group membership, and other controls determine the resulting authority.

The existence of trust is only the beginning of the analysis.

What matters is what the trusted identity can ultimately reach.
