# 2.8.3 Authority Can Be Manufactured

Identity attacks are particularly dangerous when an adversary can create new authority rather than merely steal existing authority.

A compromised user who can modify a group's membership can assign privilege.

A principal with `WriteDACL` can potentially alter who is authorized to control an object.

A principal with ownership rights may be able to change the object's access-control state.

Control of a certificate template may allow the attacker to create a credential representing another identity.

Control of a federation signing capability may permit the creation of trusted assertions.

Control over synchronization or provisioning may allow identities or attributes to be modified in a downstream system.

These mechanisms effectively manufacture authority through legitimate administrative functions.

That is why identity-security engineering cannot focus only on protecting privileged accounts.

The systems and principals capable of creating privileged accounts, assigning privileged memberships, issuing privileged credentials, or altering trusted identity state may be equally important.

In many environments, they are Tier 0 even when their names do not contain the word administrator.
