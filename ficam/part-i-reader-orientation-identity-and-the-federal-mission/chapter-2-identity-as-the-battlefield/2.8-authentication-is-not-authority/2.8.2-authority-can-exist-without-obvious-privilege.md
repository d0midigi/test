# 2.8.2 Authority Can Exist Without Obvious Privilege

Visible privilege is only one form of authority.

An account may have no administrative group membership and still possess a directory right that allows it to control a more privileged object.

A service account may not be an administrator but may execute on a server that privileged users regularly access.

A management platform's service identity may appear ordinary in the directory while the platform itself can deploy software to thousands of systems.

A backup operator may not be a Domain Admin but may possess practical control over Domain Controller recovery data.

A certificate administrator may not hold traditional directory privilege yet may influence issuance infrastructure capable of representing privileged identities.

These examples expose the limits of privilege reviews based only on group membership.

Authority can arise from object control, management reach, credential access, certificate issuance, federation signing, synchronization, backup, virtualization, or the ability to alter another security principal.

The important security property is what the identity can cause the environment to do.
