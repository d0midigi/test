# 6.5.7 Automation Does Not Eliminate Engineering Judgment

DISA supports automation content designed to help organizations apply or assess STIG requirements using configuration-management and other automation mechanisms. DISA describes its supplemental automation content as using configuration-management tooling with a STIG-centric approach while supporting customization.

Automation is essential at enterprise scale.

Manually inspecting thousands of systems against hundreds of requirements is slow, inconsistent, and difficult to sustain.

But security automation introduces a subtle risk: administrators can begin trusting the status generated by the tool more than the system being measured.

An automated check is only as accurate as the condition it evaluates.

A scanner can determine whether a configured value matches the expected value.

It may not know whether a separate policy overrides that value during actual operation.

It may verify that a privileged group contains approved members.

It may not know that another principal has permission to modify those members.

It may verify that an authentication protocol is configured securely.

It may not recognize that another authentication path provides equivalent access.

The correct relationship is therefore:

Automation establishes repeatability.

Engineering establishes meaning.

The two should reinforce one another.
