# 6.5.8 STIG Evidence Should Support RMF Rather Than Become a Separate Compliance Universe

STIG assessment produces valuable authorization evidence because it shows whether systems conform to defined DoD technical requirements.

The results should feed directly into the broader RMF risk picture.

An open STIG finding may require remediation, risk acceptance, a Plan of Action and Milestones, or another disposition according to the applicable authorization process.

Likewise, the resolution of a finding should produce evidence that the underlying technical condition has actually changed.

This is where the distinction between checklist closure and security validation becomes important.

If the finding involved weak authentication, validate the effective authentication behavior.

If the finding involved logging, generate or observe the relevant event.

If the finding involved policy, verify effective policy on the target.

If the finding involved a service or protocol, confirm that the undesired path is no longer available.

The stronger the relationship between STIG evidence and operational validation, the less likely the authorization package is to become detached from the environment it describes.

STIGs are therefore an indispensable part of DoD hardening.

They are strongest when treated as one layer of a larger security engineering model.
