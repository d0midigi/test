# 6.5.2 Baselines Must Distinguish Requirement From Implementation

A useful way to think about STIG implementation is as establishment of a known hardened state.

Without that state, security becomes dependent on defaults, historical configuration, administrator preference, or whatever settings survived the last upgrade.

That is unacceptable for identity infrastructure.

Domain Controllers should not acquire materially different security postures merely because different administrators built them.

Administrative servers should not use fundamentally different authentication policy because one was deployed three years after another.

Security settings should not be inherited accidentally from a generic workstation Group Policy because nobody created a purpose-built baseline for the systems carrying enterprise identity authority.

A STIG gives the engineering team a controlled starting point.

That does not mean every STIG setting will be operationally identical across every environment. Applicability still matters, and mission requirements can produce legitimate deviations that must be handled through the appropriate risk process.

But the burden of proof changes.

The baseline defines the expected secure state.

Deviation should require explanation.

That is significantly stronger than beginning with an arbitrary configuration and attempting to determine after the fact whether it appears secure.
