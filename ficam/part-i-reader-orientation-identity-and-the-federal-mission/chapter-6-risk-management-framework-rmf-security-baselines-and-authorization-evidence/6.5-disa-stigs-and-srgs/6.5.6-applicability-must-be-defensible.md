# 6.5.6 Applicability Must Be Defensible

STIG implementation inevitably produces applicability decisions.

Some requirements genuinely do not apply to a particular system role, technology deployment, or architecture.

That is legitimate.

The risk appears when Not Applicable becomes an administrative mechanism for removing findings rather than a technical conclusion.

Applicability should be based on evidence.

If a service is not implemented, that condition should be verifiable.

If a capability is inherited, the control provider should be identifiable.

If an authentication mechanism cannot be used by the system, the architecture should demonstrate that the path is genuinely unavailable rather than merely unused during normal operations.

This distinction is particularly important with identity because fallback behavior can create hidden applicability.

A legacy protocol may not be the preferred authentication mechanism but may remain available when Kerberos fails.

A local account may not be part of the ordinary administrative workflow but may still provide an alternate logon path.

A privileged interface may not be exposed to normal users but may remain reachable from a management network whose compromise has not been considered.

The relevant question is not whether the capability is usually used.

It is whether the capability exists and can influence the security state.
