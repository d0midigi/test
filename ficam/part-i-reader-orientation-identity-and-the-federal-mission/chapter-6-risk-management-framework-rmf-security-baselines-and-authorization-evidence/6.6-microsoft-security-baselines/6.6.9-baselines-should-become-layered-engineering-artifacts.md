# 6.6.9 Baselines Should Become Layered Engineering Artifacts

The strongest approach is therefore not to choose between DISA and Microsoft baselines but to construct a controlled hierarchy of configuration authority.

At the top sits the applicable federal and DoD security requirement.

The applicable STIG or SRG translates that requirement into DoD technical expectations.

Microsoft's security baseline contributes vendor-specific product security knowledge.

The command or program baseline incorporates the approved implementation necessary for the actual mission architecture.

Exceptions identify the places where operational requirements prevent implementation of the preferred state.

Continuous validation determines whether production still matches that approved configuration.

This produces something much more useful than a collection of hardening documents.

It produces a known security state.

For Active Directory, however, one additional layer remains necessary.

The configuration baseline must be accompanied by an authority baseline.

It is not enough to know how the Domain Controller is configured.

The defender must know who can administer it, who can alter the policies applied to it, who can control its virtualization and backup infrastructure, who can obtain authentication material accepted by it, who can replicate its sensitive directory state, and which paths allow another identity to acquire those capabilities.

DISA STIGs provide one indispensable view of that environment.

Microsoft Security Baselines provide another.

Neither can see the complete attack graph.

That final view has to be engineered from the identity relationships themselves.
