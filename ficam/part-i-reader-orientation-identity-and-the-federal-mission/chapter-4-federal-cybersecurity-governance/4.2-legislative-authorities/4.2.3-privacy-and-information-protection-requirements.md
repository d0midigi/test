# 4.2.3 Privacy and Information Protection Requirements

Identity systems contain unusually concentrated collections of information about people.

Names, identifiers, organizational affiliations, contact information, account status, group membership, authentication methods, certificates, device associations, access history, and other attributes may all exist within or around the identity system. Some identity architectures also rely on authoritative information held elsewhere and synchronize selected attributes into directories, governance platforms, applications, or cloud identity systems.

Cybersecurity therefore cannot treat identity data solely as an access-control resource.

It is also information requiring appropriate protection.

The security and privacy disciplines intersect most clearly when identity data is collected, propagated, logged, retained, and shared. A directory may require an attribute for authorization while another system may not need to receive the same attribute. Authentication telemetry may be essential for threat detection while still containing information whose retention and dissemination require governance. Identity proofing may require sensitive evidence that should not become ordinary directory data merely because it was used during enrollment.

The principle is data minimization within an operationally usable identity architecture.

The fact that an attribute exists does not mean every relying system should receive it.

The fact that an administrator can query identity information does not mean the information should be broadly exported.

The fact that security telemetry has defensive value does not remove the obligation to protect the information contained within that telemetry.

NIST's current RMF explicitly integrates security and privacy risk management rather than treating them as entirely separate life cycles.

For FICAM engineering, that integration becomes particularly important because identity systems sit at the intersection of security and personal information by design.
