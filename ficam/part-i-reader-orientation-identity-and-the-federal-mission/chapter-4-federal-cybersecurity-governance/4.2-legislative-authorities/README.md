# 4.2 Legislative Authorities

Federal cybersecurity begins with law, but law usually does not tell an Active Directory administrator how to configure a Domain Controller.

That is not a limitation.

Congress establishes obligations, authorities, responsibilities, reporting structures, and institutional mechanisms. Technical implementation is then developed through the executive, standards, departmental, and agency structures authorized to carry those requirements forward.

For the identity practitioner, legislation matters because it explains why many downstream cybersecurity processes exist at all.

Risk management, information-security programs, system inventories, control assessment, continuous monitoring, oversight, agency accountability, and federal standards are not simply conventions that cybersecurity offices adopted voluntarily.

They are part of a larger statutory and governance structure.
