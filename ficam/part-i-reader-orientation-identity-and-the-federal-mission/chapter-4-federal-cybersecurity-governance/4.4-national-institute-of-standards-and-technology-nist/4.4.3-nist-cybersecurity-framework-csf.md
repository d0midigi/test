# 4.4.3 Cybersecurity Framework

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework serves a different purpose from NIST SP 800-53.

Rather than providing a federal security-control catalog, the Cybersecurity Framework organizes cybersecurity risk-management outcomes into a structure that can be used to understand, communicate, prioritize, and improve cybersecurity capabilities.

That distinction is useful in identity security because senior leaders and identity engineers often speak at very different levels of abstraction.

A technical team may discuss Kerberos armoring, LDAP channel binding, privileged group membership, certificate-template permissions, or synchronization account rights.

Leadership may instead need to understand whether identity risks are governed, identified, protected against, detected, responded to, and recovered from.

The technical details do not disappear at the higher level.

They become evidence of a broader capability.

An identity program that can enumerate privileged access but cannot govern who should possess that access is incomplete.

A program that hardens Domain Controllers but cannot detect abuse of replication authority is incomplete.

A program that detects Golden Ticket indicators but cannot conduct trustworthy forest recovery is incomplete.

The value of the framework perspective is that it prevents one technically strong security function from being mistaken for a complete security program.

Identity resilience requires a lifecycle.
