# 4.4.4 Digital Identity Guidance

NIST's digital identity guidance is especially important to FICAM because it deals directly with the establishment and use of digital identity rather than treating identity merely as one control family among many.

The current NIST SP 800-63-4 suite addresses identity proofing, authentication and authenticator management, federation, and related digital identity functions. For this book, the most visible concepts are Identity Assurance Level (IAL), Authenticator Assurance Level (AAL), and Federation Assurance Level (FAL), but the importance of the guidance extends beyond those labels.

The assurance model separates questions that enterprise identity discussions frequently blur.

Confidence that the correct real-world subject was established is not the same problem as confidence that the subject is presenting an appropriate authenticator.

Confidence in the authenticator is not the same problem as confidence in a federated assertion passed from one system to another.

These distinctions become directly relevant to federal Active Directory environments.

A PIV or CAC authentication event may provide strong authenticator properties while the underlying account remains overprivileged.

A correctly proofed individual can still be provisioned into the wrong groups.

A strong authenticator can still be associated with a stale account.

A valid federation assertion can still be transformed into excessive downstream authorization.

The assurance framework helps ensure those security properties are evaluated separately rather than hidden behind the general phrase “strong identity.”

That separation will become increasingly important in Chapter 5, where FICAM and DoD ICAM move from governance concepts into the mechanics of identity proofing, credential binding, assurance, federation, and lifecycle management.
