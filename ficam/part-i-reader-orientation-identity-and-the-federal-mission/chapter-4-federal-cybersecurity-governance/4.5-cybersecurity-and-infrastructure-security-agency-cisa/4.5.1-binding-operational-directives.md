# 4.5.1 Binding Operational Directives

Binding Operational Directives provide a clear example of CISA's operational authority.

For agencies within their applicable FCEB scope, a Binding Operational Directive is not simply a recommendation that an agency may adopt if convenient. CISA uses BODs to direct action against cybersecurity risks considered sufficiently significant to require coordinated federal response.

That becomes relevant to Active Directory when an affected technology sits within or supports the identity trust system.

A vulnerability affecting a Domain Controller, federation service, externally exposed authentication appliance, endpoint-management platform, or certificate service may initially appear as an ordinary vulnerability-management problem.

Its identity significance may be much larger.

A remote-code-execution vulnerability in an identity authority is not equivalent to the same vulnerability on an isolated workstation.

Compromise may provide authentication material, administrative authority, directory control, or downstream trust far beyond the vulnerable host.

This is why operational vulnerability prioritization must account for identity position rather than CVSS or patch status alone.

The same software defect can represent very different mission risk depending on where the affected system sits in the trust architecture.
