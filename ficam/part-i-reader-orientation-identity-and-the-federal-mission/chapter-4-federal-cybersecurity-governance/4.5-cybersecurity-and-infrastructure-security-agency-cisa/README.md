# 4.5 Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)

The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency plays a more operational role in federal civilian cybersecurity than NIST.

Where NIST primarily develops standards, guidance, and risk-management resources, CISA has statutory and operational responsibilities for helping secure the Federal Civilian Executive Branch (FCEB), providing shared cybersecurity capabilities, issuing certain compulsory directives within its authority, coordinating vulnerability response, and producing operational guidance used across the federal enterprise. CISA also maintains federal Zero Trust implementation resources and models intended to help agencies mature their architecture.

That distinction is useful because federal practitioners often see both NIST and CISA material applied to the same system.

The documents may address related security problems while serving different purposes.

NIST may establish the risk-management or control framework.

CISA may identify an operational condition that requires urgent remediation across civilian agencies.

The identity engineer may ultimately implement both through the same technical environment.
