# 4.3.3 Office of Management and Budget

The Office of Management and Budget occupies a particularly important position because federal cybersecurity is inseparable from agency management, investment, risk, and accountability.

OMB cybersecurity memoranda can establish government-wide implementation expectations, reporting requirements, architectural objectives, and timelines for Executive Branch agencies within their scope. OMB's public memorandum repository continues to serve as the official source for such government-wide management direction.

This makes OMB especially relevant to practitioners who are accustomed to viewing cybersecurity only through technical authorities.

A security objective that requires substantial modernization is also a resource problem.

Agencies need personnel, contracts, licenses, infrastructure, modernization schedules, and budget justification.

An identity architecture cannot migrate from weak authentication to phishing-resistant authentication merely because the security team agrees that doing so would be preferable. Legacy applications may need remediation. Credential infrastructure may need replacement. Users may require new enrollment processes. Relying systems may require integration changes.

OMB's position allows federal cybersecurity direction to intersect with agency planning and resource governance in ways a technical standard alone cannot.

That does not mean OMB designs Active Directory.

It means OMB can materially influence the conditions under which agencies must modernize it.
