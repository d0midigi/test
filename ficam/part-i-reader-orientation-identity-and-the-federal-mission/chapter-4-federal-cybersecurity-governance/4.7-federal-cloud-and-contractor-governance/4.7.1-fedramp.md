# 4.7.1 FedRAMP

The Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program is a government-wide program intended to provide a standardized approach to the security assessment and authorization of cloud services used by federal agencies. FedRAMP remains under active modernization, including changes associated with its statutory authorization and the FedRAMP 20x initiative, but its core purpose continues to center on reusable cloud security assessment and risk information for federal use.

For identity engineers, FedRAMP does not make a cloud service trustworthy merely because the service has completed an authorization process.

The agency still owns the security decisions associated with how the service is integrated and used.

A cloud platform may provide strong technical controls while the agency configures federation incorrectly.

The provider may protect the underlying service while the agency grants excessive privileges to a service principal.

The platform may support phishing-resistant authentication while the agency permits a weaker legacy path for administrative access.

The security responsibility is shared because the architecture is shared.

This is particularly important in hybrid identity.

An on-premises Active Directory forest may synchronize identities into a cloud service that has its own authorization model, token system, applications, administrative roles, and trust relationships.

FedRAMP addresses the cloud service's federal security-assurance context.

It does not replace the agency's responsibility for identity architecture inside that service.
