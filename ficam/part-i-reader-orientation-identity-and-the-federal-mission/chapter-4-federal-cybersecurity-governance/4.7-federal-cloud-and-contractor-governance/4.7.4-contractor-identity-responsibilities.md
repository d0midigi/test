# 4.7.4 Contractor Identity Responsibilities

Contractor identity governance should therefore be treated as part of federal identity architecture rather than as a separate human-resources problem.

The relevant issue is the authority the identity possesses.

A contractor serving as a Tier 0 administrator requires Tier 0 protection.

A contractor maintaining an application with no privileged access should not inherit broad directory authority simply because the contract supports IT.

A contractor account should have a sponsor, purpose, lifecycle, entitlement model, and termination path appropriate to the access being granted.

The most dangerous contractor identities are often not those deliberately granted high privilege.

They are identities whose original purpose changed while access remained.

A developer temporarily receives production access.

A support engineer receives membership in an administrative group during an outage.

A contractor account is retained between task orders.

A service identity created by a vendor remains after the application is migrated.

These conditions are ordinary examples of identity debt.

The fact that the identity originated through a contract does not change the security problem.

Unreviewed authority remains unreviewed authority.
