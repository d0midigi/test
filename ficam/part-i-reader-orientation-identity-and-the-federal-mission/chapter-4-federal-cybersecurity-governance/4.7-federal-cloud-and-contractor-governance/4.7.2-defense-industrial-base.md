# 4.7.2 Defense Industrial Base

The Defense Industrial Base presents another distributed trust problem.

Defense missions depend extensively on nonfederal organizations that develop systems, provide services, manufacture components, process government information, and participate in the defense supply chain. NSA's Cybersecurity Collaboration Center explicitly identifies hardening the Defense Industrial Base as part of its cybersecurity collaboration mission.

Identity is a major part of that dependency because contractor personnel may require government access while contractor-operated systems may themselves contain Federal Contract Information (FCI), Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI), or credentials and applications supporting defense programs.

The resulting trust works in both directions.

The government must control what contractor identities may access.

The contractor must protect the systems in which government information and associated identities operate.

A compromised contractor identity can become a government access problem.

A compromised contractor network can expose credentials used against government services.

An unmanaged sponsored account can remain active after the underlying contract relationship changes.

A third-party application integration can retain access long after its original purpose has ended.

Supply-chain identity security therefore includes lifecycle management, not merely authentication strength.
