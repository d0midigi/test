# 4.6.6 Military Departments and Service Components

Department-level governance ultimately has to be implemented by organizations operating real systems.

Military Departments, Defense Agencies, combatant commands, field activities, program offices, enterprise service providers, and local commands inherit department requirements while operating under different missions and technical architectures.

This is where cybersecurity becomes operationally specific.

A policy that appears straightforward at enterprise scale may become difficult when applied to a disconnected tactical enclave.

A privileged-access standard may require adaptation for a mission system with specialized administration.

A certificate dependency may be shared across several commands whose maintenance windows do not align.

A trust relationship may exist because a combatant command requires mission-partner interoperability that cannot simply be removed by the local Active Directory team.

These constraints do not nullify department policy.

They explain why RMF includes risk decisions and why engineering requires mission context.

Local implementation is where abstract governance encounters the actual terrain.
