# 4.6.3 National Security Agency

The National Security Agency contributes a different form of cybersecurity authority and expertise.

NSA's cybersecurity mission focuses on protecting National Security Systems and reducing threats to national-security interests, while its Cybersecurity Collaboration Center works with industry, interagency, and international partners and has particular emphasis on the Defense Industrial Base.

For identity practitioners, NSA guidance often becomes especially relevant when the issue involves cryptography, authentication, privileged-access security, nation-state tradecraft, or technical hardening of high-value systems.

The distinction between NIST and NSA is therefore not simply civilian versus military.

Their missions and authorities differ.

NIST develops broadly applicable cybersecurity standards and guidance.

NSA brings national-security mission, cryptographic, signals-intelligence-derived, and threat-informed expertise to defense of highly sensitive environments.

In practice, DoD engineering may consume both.

A certificate architecture may need to satisfy federal standards, DoD implementation policy, platform-specific requirements, and cryptographic guidance associated with National Security Systems.

A privileged-access design may be informed by general Zero Trust principles while being hardened according to threats observed against the DODIN.

The engineer's task is not to collapse those sources into a single undifferentiated list.

It is to understand what each contributes to the final security design.
