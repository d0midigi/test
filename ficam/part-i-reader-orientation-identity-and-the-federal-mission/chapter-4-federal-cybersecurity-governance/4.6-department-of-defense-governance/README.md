# 4.6 Department of Defense Governance

Department of Defense identity environments operate within the broader federal cybersecurity structure while adding departmental policy, mission requirements, operational command relationships, technical implementation guidance, and National Security System considerations.

The result is not a parallel universe separate from NIST.

DoD security engineering draws heavily from common federal risk-management foundations while applying them through department-specific governance, architectures, technical baselines, cyber operations, and mission structures. DISA's own STIG program explicitly describes STIGs and Security Requirements Guides as bridging NIST SP 800-53 and RMF requirements into technical guidance for DoD information technologies.

For practitioners, the most useful way to understand this environment is functionally.

Different organizations answer different parts of the security problem.

The DoD CIO establishes department-level information enterprise and cybersecurity policy and strategy.

DISA provides enterprise services and develops or operates important implementation capabilities, including the STIG/SRG ecosystem.

NSA contributes national-security cybersecurity expertise, cryptographic expertise, threat knowledge, and technical guidance.

USCYBERCOM conducts cyberspace operations and directs defense of the Department of Defense Information Network (DODIN) through its operational structure.

JFHQ-DODIN provides command and control for DODIN operations and defensive activities.

Military Departments, Defense Agencies, combatant commands, and local organizations then operate systems within this larger governance and operational framework.

For an Active Directory engineer sitting at a keyboard, these distinctions can appear far removed from a Group Policy setting.

They are not.

They explain why the setting exists, who can direct change, what threat information influences prioritization, how compliance is assessed, and what happens when an identity weakness becomes an operational cybersecurity event.
