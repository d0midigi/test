# 4.6.4 United States Cyber Command

United States Cyber Command operates at the cyberspace-operations layer.

USCYBERCOM's public mission material identifies defense of the DODIN as a core mission and describes the Department's classified and unclassified networks as supporting day-to-day DoD operations.

This introduces a distinction that becomes important to identity operations: governance determines how systems should be secured, while cyber operations must contend with adversaries actively attempting to compromise them.

An identity weakness may begin life as a configuration problem.

Once an adversary exploits it, the problem changes.

A vulnerable administrative relationship becomes an operational attack path.

A compromised privileged credential becomes an incident.

A federation weakness becomes a possible mechanism for adversary access.

A directory replication anomaly may become evidence of domain compromise.

This operational perspective is one reason threat-informed defense matters in DoD environments. Security requirements cannot remain static descriptions of ideal configuration. They have to be understood against adversaries who continuously adapt their methods to the controls being deployed.
