# Author's Note to Reader on Responsible Use

The offensive material in _Contested Terrain_ is included because defenders cannot adequately protect identity infrastructure they do not understand from an adversary's perspective. Throughout my career, some of the most important security lessons I learned came from examining what an attacker could do with configurations that appeared routine, compliant, or operationally harmless.

That knowledge carries great power and responsibility.

The techniques presented in this book are intended for authorized security assessment, laboratory research, adversary emulation, defensive validation, and the engineering of more resilient identity environments. They are not permission to access, manipulate, disrupt, or test systems for which the reader has not been explicitly authorized.

Federal and military cybersecurity practitioners operate in environments where technical actions can have consequences far beyond the computer immediately in front of them. Identity infrastructure may support production mission systems, partner environments, operational, tactical, and ballistics networks, and personnel whose ability to authenticate is itself a mission dependency.

Learn the offensive tradecraft. Understand it with a passion and deeply. Reproduce it in your lab. Use it during authorized assessments where the Rules of Engagement (RoE) and your "get out of jail card" (e.g., signed authorization form from system or network owner) permit it. Then use that knowledge for the reason it is provided here to identify where trust can fail before an actual adversary does.
