# 1.7 The Reader Contract

A technical book implicitly makes promises to its reader. Contested Terrain makes those promises explicit because the subject sits at an unusual intersection of offensive tradecraft, defensive engineering, federal governance, and mission operations.

The book will not simplify a technical mechanism merely because the full explanation is inconvenient. At the same time, complexity will not be introduced for display. Depth is useful only when it improves the reader's ability to understand, assess, attack, defend, detect, or recover the identity system.

Where a concept is historically important but no longer represents preferred architecture, that distinction will be made. Legacy technologies remain relevant because production Active Directory environments continue to contain them, but historical prevalence will not be presented as current best practice.

Similarly, security guidance will be tied to the authority appropriate to the environment. Federal policy, National Institute of Standards and Technology guidance, Department of Defense ICAM direction, Defense Information Systems Agency implementation requirements, Microsoft platform behavior, and operational security guidance serve different purposes. Treating them as interchangeable weakens the analysis.

The reader should expect the book to preserve those boundaries.
