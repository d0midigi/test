# 1.7.1 What This Book Is

Contested Terrain is an identity-security engineering reference built around federal Active Directory environments.

That description is deliberately broader than "Active Directory security book."

The directory remains central, but the security boundary includes the systems capable of establishing, extending, synchronizing, certifying, federating, administering, or recovering identity trust. A Certification Authority that can mint authentication certificates matters to domain security. An Entra Connect server capable of synchronizing privileged identity state matters to domain security. A virtualization platform capable of controlling Domain Controllers matters to domain security. A federation service whose signing keys establish trusted assertions matters to domain security.

The book therefore follows authority wherever it actually exists rather than stopping at a product boundary.

It is also deliberately both offensive and defensive. The two perspectives are used to validate one another. Offensive tradecraft exposes the consequences of design and configuration. Defensive engineering attempts to remove or contain those consequences. Detection determines whether the environment can recognize abuse. Recovery determines whether compromised authority can be trusted again.

FICAM provides the federal identity context tying those activities together.
