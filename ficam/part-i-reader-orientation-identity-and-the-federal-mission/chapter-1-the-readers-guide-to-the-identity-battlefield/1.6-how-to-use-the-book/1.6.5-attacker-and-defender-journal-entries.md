# 1.6.5 Attacker and Defender Journal Entries

The Attacker and Defender Journal Entries are used to expose the reasoning that often disappears from formal technical descriptions.

A tool output can show that an account is vulnerable to AS-REP Roasting. It cannot, by itself, show why an operator selected that account instead of another one, what other pathways were considered, what environmental detail made the account interesting, or attractive, or what the defender could have noticed before exploitation occurred.

The attack entry follows the progression from observation to decision. It may begin with something mundane: an attribute, a session, a naming convention, a certificate template, an unusual permission, or a service account. The significance develops as that observation is connected to the surrounding identity architecture.

The defender entry examines the same condition from the opposing direction. Instead of beginning with the final alert, it asks what engineering or visibility failure allowed the condition to persist. In some cases, the most useful defensive lesson will not be a new detection at all. It may be better lifecycle governance, stronger delegation boundaries, removal of a stale service account, reduction of standing privileges, or correction of an authentication design that should never have produced the opportunity in the first place.

These entities are deliberately more operational than the surrounding reference material. They are intended to show how practitioners actually think while working through incomplete information.
