# 1.6.8 Offensive and Defensive Tooling

Tools are introduced according to the function they perform rather than their reputation within the security community.

BloodHound matters because it models relationships and exposes effective control paths. SharpHound matters because it collects the directory and endpoint information required to construct those relationships. Rubeus matters because it exposes Kerberos operations. Certipy and Certify matter because they allow certificate-service architecture to be enumerated and exercised. Impacket matters because its implementations of Windows protocols make many directory, authentication, and remote-management operations accessible for testing.

The defensive side follows the same principle. Microsoft Defender for Identity, Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) platforms, native Windows auditing, PowerShell, directory administrative tools, PKI consoles, Group Policy Management, and other utilities are discussed according to the evidence or control they provide.

No tool should be mistaken for the discipline itself.

An agency can purchase identity-security products and still maintain dangerous control paths. A penetration tester can possess every common Active Directory tool and still misunderstand the environment. A defender can collect enormous amounts of telemetry and still fail to recognize a meaningful identity compromise.

Tools extend practitioner capability. They do not replace architectural understanding.
