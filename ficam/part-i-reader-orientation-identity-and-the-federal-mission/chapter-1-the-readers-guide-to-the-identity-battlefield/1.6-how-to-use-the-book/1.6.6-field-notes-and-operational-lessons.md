# 1.6.6 Field Notes and Operational Lessons

Not every important lesson fits neatly into architecture, exploitation, or control guidance.

Production identity environments accumulate behaviors that are technically explainable but operationally messy. An old service account may remain because nobody knows what will break if it is removed. A Group Policy Object may exist for a reason no current administrator can identify. A certificate template may be widely used even though no team claims ownership of it. A forest trust is created for a migration may remain years after the migration ended. An account marked as "temporary" suddenly becomes a permanent fixture within the domain because an application dependency grew around it.

These are not edge cases. They are normal characteristics of long-lived enterprise infrastructure.

Field Notes are where those realities are surfaced directly. They provide space to discuss the gap between how identity architecture is documented and how it often exists in real-world operations.

The purpose is not cynicism. Mature security engineering depends on recognizing that system accumulate histories. A technically elegant recommendation that ignores that history may fail because it cannot be implemented safely. The better practitioner understands both the target security state and the operational pathway required to reach it.
