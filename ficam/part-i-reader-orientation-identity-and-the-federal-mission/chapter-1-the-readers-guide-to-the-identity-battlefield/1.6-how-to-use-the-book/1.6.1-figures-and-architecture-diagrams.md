# 1.6.1 Figures and Architecture Diagrams

Identity infrastructure is difficult to understand through prose alone because so much of its security meaning exists in tangled relationships. Forests contain domains. Domains contain objects. Objects possess permissions over other objects. Kerberos clients communicate with Key Distribution Centers (KDS) and related services. Certificate authorities issue credentials that are later consumed by authenticating systems. Federation moves assertions between security boundaries. Hybrid identity systems synchronize selected state between directories that are administered solely through different control planes.

A diagram often reveals relationships that several pages of prose can obscure.

Figures in this book are intended to do more than decorate the surrounding text. They serve as technical models. Where appropriate, diagrams identify trust direction, authentication flow, administrative authority, protocol exchanges, certificate chains, control pathways, or the sequence through which an adversary converts one form of access into another.

The same architecture may also appear more than once from a different perspective. A forest trust diagram used in the architecture portion of the book may later be redrawn as an attack-path diagram. A Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) hierarchy may first show legitimate certificate issuance and later show where certificate enrollment, mapping, or Certification Authority (CA) control can subvert authentication. A hybrid identity diagram may initially describe synchronization and later identify the systems whose compromise bridges on-premises and cloud authority.

This is intentional. The infrastructure has not changed. The analytical perspective has.

Readers should treat those figures as part of the technical content rather than as summaries to glance at and skip.
