# 1.1.2 Why Offensive and Defensive Security Belong Together

The separation between offense and defense is useful organizationally, but technically the two disciplines study the same mechanisms.

A discretionary access control list is not inherently offensive or defensive.

Kerberos is not offensive or defensive.

An Active Directory Certificate Services (AD CS) certificate template is not offensive or defensive.

A forest trust is not offensive or defensive.

A Group Policy Object is not offensive or defensive.

The security consequence depends on who controls the mechanism, how it is configured, what authority it conveys, and whether defenders can determine precisely when that authority is being abused.

An attacker studies a domain trust relationship to determine whether it creates a pathway.

A defender studies the same relationship to determine whether that path should exist.

An attacker examines certificate enrollment rights to determine whether a certificate can be obtained for an identity the attacker should not control.

A defender examines the same enrollment rights to determine whether the issuing architecture restricts authentication authority correctly.

An attacker enumerates group membership to identify privilege.

A defender evaluates the same membership to determine whether the entitlement is necessary, approved, monitored, and periodically recertified.

The underlying evidence is often identical.

The question being asked of it is vastly different.

For that reason, this book does not treat offensive tradecraft as a collection of tricks that defenders merely need to recognize. Offensive knowledge is used as a validation mechanism for security engineering and as a defensive discipline.

If a control is intended to stop a particular attack pathway, the defender should understand the attack pathway well enough to determine whether the control actually breaks it.

If a remedial effort closes one edge but creates another, the environment has not necessarily become more secure.

If a security baseline is technically satisfied but an attacker can still reach equivalent authority through an indirect relationship, compliance and effective security have diverged.

That distinction will appear repeatedly throughout this book.
