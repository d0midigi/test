# 1.1.3 Why the Federal Context Changes the Problem

Active Directory does not become a different Microsoft product when it is deployed inside a federal agency or military command.

The surrounding trust system changes, however.

Federal identity environments may incorporate PIV and CAC credentials, Federal Public Key Infrastructure (FPKI), identity proofing requirements, digital identity assurance, mission-partner federation, cross-domain architectures, multiple security enclaves, formal authorization boundaries, privileged access controls, operational readiness requirements, continuous monitoring, and other dependencies that are not usually central to a commercial Active Directory text.

The identity engineer therefore cannot stop at the domain boundary.

Neither can the attacker.

A compromised directory object may affect certificate authentication.

A certificate-service configuration may create directory authority.

A synchronization platform may bridge on-premises and cloud privilege.

A federation relationship may extend trust beyond the forest.

A privileged management platform may control systems that themselves administer identity infrastructure.

A mission-partner relationship may permit an identity assertion originating outside the local administrative boundary to influence access within it.

The resulting security terrain is larger than Active Directory Domain Services (AD DS), even when AD DS remains at its center.

_Contested Terrain_ uses that larger identity trust system as its unit of analysis.
