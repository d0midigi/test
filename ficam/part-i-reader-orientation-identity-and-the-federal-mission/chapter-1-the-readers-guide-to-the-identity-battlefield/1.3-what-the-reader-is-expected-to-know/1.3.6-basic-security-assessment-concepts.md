# 1.3.6 Basic Security Assessment Concepts

Readers should understand that professional security testing is governed by authorization, scope, operational constraints, and evidence requirements.

This becomes particularly important in federal and military environments where an assessment may involve production systems supporting mission-essential functions, classified or sensitive data, mission partners, external service providers, or tightly controlled maintenance windows.

An operator cannot assume that because a technique is technically safe in a laboratory, it is operationally acceptable in a production Command environment.

Password-spray testing may trigger lockouts.

Certificate revocation may interrupt authentication.

A malformed directory modification may cause replication consequences.

Group Policy changes may affect hundreds or thousands of systems.

Testing Kerberos recovery procedures may invalidate existing authentication sessions.

Cloud Conditional Access modifications may block mission users.

Federation changes may affect partner connectivity.

The professional security practitioner must therefore understand the difference between capability and authority.

Being capable of executing a technique does not grant permission to execute it.

Later chapters provide detailed assessment methodology, but the reader should begin with the principle that all offensive activity requires explicit authorization, defined Rules of Engagement (RoE), scope awareness, operational deconfliction, and an understanding of potential mission impact.

In federal security work, technical competence includes knowing when not to execute a technically valid action.
