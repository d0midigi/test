# 1.4.7 You Do Not Need Prior Federal Experience

Federal cybersecurity has its own operational vocabulary, institutional structure, evidence requirements, and risk-management processes. To someone entering that environment for the first time, those concepts can feel like an additional technical stack layered on top of an already complex job.

The reader is not expected to know that vocabulary before beginning.

Terms such as Authorizing Official, ISSO, ISSM, Information Systems Security Engineer (ISSE), Security Control Assessor, Cybersecurity Service Provider (CSSP), eMASS, STIG, Security Requirements Guide, IAVA, POA\&M, continuous monitoring, authorization boundary, mission assurance, and common control will be introduced in context.

More importantly, they will be connected to technical reality.

The reader will not merely learn that eMASS stores authorization evidence. The reader will learn what identity-security evidence must be produced.

The reader will not merely learn that STIGs establish configuration requirements. The reader will see how those requirements influence Domain Controller configuration, Group Policy, authentication, auditing, privileged access, certificate infrastructure, and administrative workstations.

The reader will not merely learn that RMF includes a Monitor step. The reader will examine what continuous monitoring of identity infrastructure actually requires.

The goal is to remove the artificial divide between "technical security" and "federal compliance."

In operational environments, the two continuously interact.
