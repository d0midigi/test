# 1.4.5 You Do Not Need to Know FICAM or DoD ICAM

Readers coming from commercial Active Directory environments may initially be unfamiliar with FICAM, Department of Defense Identity, Credential, and Access Management (DoD ICAM), Personal Identity Verification, Common Access Cards, the Electronic Data Interchange Personal Identifier (EDIPI), assurance levels, authoritative attributes, federal identity proofing, or mission-partner federation.

The book assumes no prior expertise in those areas.

However, it treats them as core architecture rather than compliance trivia.

The reason is simple: federal identity security cannot be understood solely by studying Microsoft directory mechanics.

Before a domain account exists, a human identity may have been sponsored, proofed, validated, enrolled, credentialed, and bound to an authoritative identity record. The resulting credential may carry certificates issued through a federal PKI trust chain. That identity may authenticate to Active Directory, federate to another agency or mission partner, receive access based on authoritative attributes, and later be reviewed or revoked through lifecycle governance.

An adversary does not necessarily attack all of those steps.

But an adversary may benefit from weaknesses anywhere along that trust chain.

A stolen credential, incorrect certificate mapping, unauthorized attribute modification, stale account, federation error, synchronization failure, or lifecycle breakdown can create access even when the upstream identity proofing process was performed correctly.

For that reason, the book teaches the federal identity model before repeatedly testing whether the technical implementation actually preserves the trust that model expects.
