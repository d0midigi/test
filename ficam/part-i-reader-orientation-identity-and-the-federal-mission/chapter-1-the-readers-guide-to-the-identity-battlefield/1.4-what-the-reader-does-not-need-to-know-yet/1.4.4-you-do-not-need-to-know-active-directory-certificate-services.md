# 1.4.4 You Do Not Need to Know Active Directory Certificate Services

Active Directory Certificate Services (AD CS) often appears in offensive-security discussions as a list of escalation classifications identified as ESC1 through ESC16.

Those classifications are useful, but they are not a substitute for understanding Public Key Infrastructure.

Before the book teaches certificate-service abuse, it establishes what certificates actually represent and why systems trust them.

Readers will learn certificate chains, root and subordinate Certification Authorities, issuing CAs, certificate templates, enrollment rights, private keys, Subject Alternative Names (SANs), Enhanced Key Usage, application policies, certificate mapping, NTAuth, Certificate Revocation Lists (CRLs), Online Certificate Status Protocol (OCSP), Authority Information Access (AIA), certificate distribution points, and related PKI components.

This foundation changes how the offensive material is interpreted.

ESC1 is not important because somebody assigned it the label "ESC1."

It is important because a particular combination of template configuration, enrollment permission, subject-name control, authentication capability, and CA trust can allow one principal to obtain a certificate that represents another identity.

The vulnerability is a trust-design failure expressed through certificate infrastructure.

That is much more valuable knowledge than memorizing an ESC number.
