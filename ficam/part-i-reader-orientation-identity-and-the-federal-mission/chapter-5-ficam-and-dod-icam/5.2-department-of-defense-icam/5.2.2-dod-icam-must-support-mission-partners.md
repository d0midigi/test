# 5.2.2 DoD ICAM Must Support Mission Partners

Mission-partner access is not an edge case in DoD identity architecture.

The DoD Enterprise ICAM Reference Design explicitly accounts for mission-partner entities whose identities, attributes, credentials, authentication, and federation services may originate outside the Department. The design allows those capabilities to be provided internally or through external federated service providers depending on the use case.

That creates a different identity-security problem from ordinary workforce access.

A DoD employee can often be governed through DoD-controlled identity and credential processes.

A coalition partner, allied military member, contractor, interagency participant, or another external mission entity may remain governed by an external identity authority.

DoD must then decide what portions of that external identity it is willing to trust and under what conditions.

The resulting access relationship requires two independent forms of confidence.

The first concerns the upstream identity: the extent to which the external authority can be trusted to establish, authenticate, maintain, and revoke its identity correctly.

The second concerns local authorization: the extent to which DoD resources constrain that identity to the exact authority required for the mission.

A failure in either side can produce excessive access.

This makes federation governance inseparable from mission-partner security.
