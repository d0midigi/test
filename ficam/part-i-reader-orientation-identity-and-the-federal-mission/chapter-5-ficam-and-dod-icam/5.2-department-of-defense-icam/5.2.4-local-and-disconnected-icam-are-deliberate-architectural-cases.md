# 5.2.4 Local and Disconnected ICAM Are Deliberate Architectural Cases

Enterprise identity services are most effective when connectivity to those services is reliable.

DoD missions cannot always make that assumption.

The DoD Enterprise ICAM Reference Design explicitly recognizes that Component, Community of Interest, or local ICAM services may be necessary when enterprise services are unavailable or do not satisfy mission requirements. It gives coalition environments with little or no NIPRNet connectivity as an example of where local identity registration and credentialing may be required.

This is not a failure of enterprise ICAM.

It is an acknowledgement of operational reality.

Identity systems operating under disconnected, intermittent, or constrained connectivity must preserve enough local authority to continue the mission while avoiding uncontrolled divergence from enterprise identity state.

That tradeoff is difficult.

The more autonomy the local system possesses, the greater the possibility that credential, attribute, or lifecycle state becomes stale.

The less autonomy it possesses, the greater the chance that loss of enterprise connectivity becomes loss of mission access.

A secure design cannot pretend that this tension does not exist.

It has to define how trust degrades.
