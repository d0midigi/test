# 5.2.3 DoD ICAM Includes Person and Non-Person Entities

One of the most important architectural features of the DoD model is that identity is not limited to people.

The reference design explicitly includes both person entities and Non-Person Entities (NPEs). It also describes DoD Component responsibility for implementing ICAM across employees, contractors, mission-partner entities, information systems, and resources and for using enterprise ICAM capabilities for NPEs where available.

This matters because enterprise security programs historically govern human identities more rigorously than machine or service identities.

Human accounts typically have sponsors.

They are tied to employment or assignment.

They have recognizable owners.

They may be periodically reviewed.

Service and machine identities frequently evolve differently.

A service account is created for an application.

The application is upgraded.

Another service begins using the same credential.

The original administrator leaves.

The account remains because nobody knows every dependency attached to it.

A machine certificate is provisioned automatically.

An automation identity gains additional API permissions.

A workload identity is granted access to a cloud resource and later reused by another application.

These identities can accumulate authority without the visibility applied to human users.

DoD ICAM's inclusion of NPEs is therefore not merely taxonomic.

It identifies an identity-governance problem that has direct offensive consequences.
