# 5.7 Credential Governance

Credential governance begins where identity governance leaves off.

Once an identity has been established, the enterprise must control which authentication mechanisms are allowed to represent it, how those mechanisms are issued, where they can be used, how they are protected, and how they are revoked.

The problem has become more complex as enterprise authentication has diversified.

A federal identity may possess a PIV or CAC, domain password, derived credential, device-bound key, FIDO authenticator, certificate, cloud authentication method, recovery mechanism, and one or more active sessions. These mechanisms differ technically, but they all participate in the same fundamental function: allowing a relying system to accept activity as originating from the trusted identity.

Credential governance therefore cannot be organized around password policy alone.
