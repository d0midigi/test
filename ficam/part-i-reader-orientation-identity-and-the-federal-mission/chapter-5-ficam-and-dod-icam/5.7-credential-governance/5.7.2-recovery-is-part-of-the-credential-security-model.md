# 5.7.2 Recovery Is Part of the Credential Security Model

Authenticator recovery is frequently designed under usability pressure.

Users lose devices.

Cards fail.

PINs are forgotten.

Hardware is replaced.

Personnel travel.

Accounts become locked.

The enterprise needs a practical way to restore access.

That recovery path may be weaker than the normal authentication mechanism if it is not deliberately engineered.

A system can require phishing-resistant multifactor authentication during ordinary use and then permit account recovery through a help-desk process based on easily obtained personal information.

The result is not phishing-resistant identity security.

It is strong routine authentication with a weak administrative bypass.

Federal credential governance should therefore treat recovery as an alternate issuance event.

The process is re-establishing the user's ability to authenticate.

That authority should be protected according to the consequences of the credential being replaced.

This is especially important for privileged identities. A weak reset path to a Tier 0 administrator can negate the protections surrounding the administrator's normal authenticator.

The book returns to this issue during account lifecycle hardening because recovery mechanisms are increasingly attractive targets as authentication protocols themselves become stronger.
