# 5.7.1 Strong Credentials Require Strong Enrollment

Security teams naturally focus on the point where an authenticator is used.

Attackers increasingly focus on the point where an authenticator is added.

If phishing-resistant authentication prevents an adversary from stealing a credential through a conventional phishing page, registering a new attacker-controlled authenticator to the victim's account may accomplish the same objective through a different path.

This is why credential binding and recovery are part of the attack surface.

An authentication mechanism is only as trustworthy as the process through which the enterprise decided that it belonged to the user.

The strongest cryptographic authenticator in the environment can become adversary-controlled if the registration workflow permits unauthorized binding.

The defensive requirement is therefore twofold: protect the authenticator and protect the authority to create or replace authenticators.

This is analogous to Active Directory permissions more broadly. Protecting an administrative password is insufficient if another principal can reset the password whenever desired.

Protecting a smart card is insufficient if an alternate credential can be registered without equivalent assurance.

Credential governance has to protect the mechanisms that manufacture authentication capability.
