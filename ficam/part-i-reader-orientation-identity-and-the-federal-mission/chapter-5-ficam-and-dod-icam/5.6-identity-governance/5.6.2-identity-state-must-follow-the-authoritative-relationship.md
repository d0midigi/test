# 5.6.2 Identity State Must Follow the Authoritative Relationship

The most important identity lifecycle events are often not creation and deletion.

They are changes.

A user transfers from one command to another.

An administrator leaves the infrastructure team but remains employed.

A contractor moves from development to sustainment.

A service is migrated to another platform.

A device changes ownership.

An application becomes mission-essential after years of low-impact use.

If identity governance recognizes only active and inactive states, these changes are difficult to represent safely.

The identity may remain valid while its previous authorization is no longer appropriate.

This is why the Joiner-Mover-Leaver model is valuable but should not be interpreted too narrowly.

The mover is often the hardest state.

Joiners attract attention because accounts have to be created.

Leavers attract attention because access has to be terminated.

Movers can accumulate access silently.

The account remains valid, so nothing forces the system to reconsider the authority it already possesses.

Over time, this creates entitlement accumulation.

For privileged users, that accumulation can produce direct attack paths.

For ordinary users, it can create lateral access across systems that no longer correspond to their mission responsibilities.

Identity governance must therefore respond to authoritative change, not merely account creation and termination.
