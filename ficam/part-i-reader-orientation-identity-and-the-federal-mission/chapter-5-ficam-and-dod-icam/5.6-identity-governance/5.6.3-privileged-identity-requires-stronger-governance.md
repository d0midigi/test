# 5.6.3 Privileged Identity Requires Stronger Governance

Privileged identities should not be governed as ordinary workforce accounts that happen to belong to more groups.

Their authority creates different consequences and therefore requires more deliberate lifecycle control.

A privileged account should have an identifiable purpose.

Its relationship to the underlying person identity should be maintained.

Its authorization should correspond to an administrative function.

Its use should be limited to appropriate systems and administrative contexts.

Its existence should be periodically justified.

This becomes more complicated as privileged access shifts toward time-bound models.

A user may possess no standing membership in a privileged group while remaining eligible to activate that role through Privileged Identity Management or another approval system.

Traditional access reviews that inspect only current group membership can therefore underestimate actual authority.

Eligibility is authority that has not yet been activated.

Governance must understand both.

This distinction becomes increasingly important in hybrid environments where cloud roles, application permissions, privileged identity workflows, and on-premises directory groups may all contribute to administrative reach.
