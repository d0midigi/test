# 5.6.1 Identity Governance Is the Control Against Identity Debt

The identity debt introduced earlier in this book often begins as a governance failure before it becomes a technical weakness.

A temporary account has no expiration.

A contractor identity remains after contract completion.

An administrative account has no identifiable owner.

A user changes duties while retaining the previous role's groups.

A service identity exists without a responsible application owner.

A synchronized account remains active because nobody knows which system is authoritative for deactivation.

Each of these cases eventually appears in Active Directory as technical state.

The root cause may have occurred much earlier.

Governance provides the mechanism for preventing identity state from becoming permanent merely because nobody explicitly removed it.

This requires ownership.

Every durable identity should have an authoritative reason for existence. Human identities require a valid workforce, sponsorship, mission-partner, or comparable relationship. Service identities require an operational owner and documented function. Device identities require a valid relationship to managed assets. Application and workload identities require lifecycle ownership associated with the systems they support.

The precise governance process differs by entity type.

The principle does not.

Unowned identity becomes unmanaged authority.
