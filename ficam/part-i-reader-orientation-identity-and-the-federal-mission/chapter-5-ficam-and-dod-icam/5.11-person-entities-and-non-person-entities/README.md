# 5.11 Person Entities and Non-Person Entities

Enterprise identity was historically designed primarily around people.

The mental model remains visible in common terminology: users authenticate, users belong to groups, users request access, users reset passwords, and users are terminated when they leave the organization.

Modern enterprises contain enormous populations of identities that are not people.

Computers authenticate.

Services authenticate.

Applications authenticate.

Automation authenticates.

Cloud workloads authenticate.

Network devices authenticate.

Software agents authenticate.

Robotic processes authenticate.

Management platforms authenticate to other systems in order to perform administrative work.

These identities may never interact with a logon screen, never possess a human-readable job title, and never participate in a traditional workforce lifecycle. They can nevertheless possess more effective authority than many human administrators.

FICAM and DoD ICAM therefore cannot treat Non-Person Entities (NPEs) as exceptions around a fundamentally human identity model.

They are part of the identity system.

The challenge is that the controls used to govern people do not always translate cleanly.
