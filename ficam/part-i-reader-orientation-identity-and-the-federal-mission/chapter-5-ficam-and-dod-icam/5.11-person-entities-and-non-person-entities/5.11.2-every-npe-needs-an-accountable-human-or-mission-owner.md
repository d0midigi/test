# 5.11.2 Every NPE Needs an Accountable Human or Mission Owner

Non-person identity does not mean ownerless identity.

A service may execute automatically, but someone is responsible for the service.

An application may authenticate programmatically, but some team owns the application.

A workload may be ephemeral, but some platform or mission process creates it.

An automation account may never be used interactively, but some operational function depends on its authority.

The identity-governance model should preserve that ownership.

This does not mean assigning a person's name permanently to every service account and hoping the spreadsheet remains current. Ownership itself needs a lifecycle. If the application changes teams, the ownership relationship must change with it.

The objective is accountability.

When an NPE is discovered with dangerous privilege, the organization should be able to determine why it has that privilege, which function requires it, which systems depend on it, how its credential is maintained, and who has authority to approve change.

Without that context, security teams become reluctant to remediate the identity because the operational consequences are unknown.

Uncertainty becomes a form of persistence.
