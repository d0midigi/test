# 5.11.4 NPE Privilege Can Be Difficult to See

Human privilege is often visible through familiar administrative roles.

Domain Admin.

Enterprise Admin.

Server Administrator.

Database Administrator.

NPE privilege may be expressed through application permissions and indirect system relationships instead.

A synchronization account might possess directory replication-related rights.

A backup service may have access to protected system state.

A deployment agent may execute under high local privilege across thousands of endpoints.

A service principal may have application permissions that allow tenant-wide data access.

A management service may possess rights to configure systems that ordinary administrators cannot reach directly.

Looking only at group membership can make these identities appear ordinary.

Their privilege is functional.

What can the service do?

Where can it authenticate?

What can it modify?

What credentials can it obtain?

What systems trust its output?

This is the same effective-authority analysis applied to NPEs.

The identity's name may be unremarkable.

Its control plane may not be.
