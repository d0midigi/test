# 5.9.1 Federation Transfers an Identity Decision, Not Unlimited Authority

The most important governance boundary in federation is the separation between authentication and local authorization.

The identity provider establishes the authenticated identity and provides whatever claims or attributes have been agreed upon.

The relying party determines what that identity is permitted to do locally.

This allows the external identity to remain externally governed without giving the external organization administrative control over the relying application.

The distinction must remain explicit.

If an application simply converts a broad upstream claim into powerful local authorization without sufficient constraint, federation can magnify an upstream error.

Suppose an identity provider asserts an organizational role.

The relying application maps that role automatically into local administrative access.

The application has made the upstream attribute part of its own administrative boundary.

That may be appropriate.

If so, the integrity, lifecycle, and governance of the upstream attribute now matter to local security.

The relying party cannot claim that authorization remains purely local while importing the fact that determines authorization from elsewhere.

Federation governance exists partly to make these dependencies visible.
