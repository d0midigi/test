# 5.9.3 Federation Expands the Consequence of Signing-Key Compromise

Federation also demonstrates how concentrated trust changes incident severity.

A normal user credential represents one identity.

A federation signing key can participate in establishing trust for many identities and potentially many relying applications.

Compromise of that signing authority can therefore create a qualitatively different problem from compromise of one user account.

The attacker may be able to manufacture identity assertions that are accepted because they are cryptographically associated with an authority the relying party already trusts.

The technical consequences depend on protocol, implementation, key architecture, relying-party configuration, and other controls, but the security principle is broader:

The more identities an authority can represent, the more strategically important protection of that authority becomes.

This same principle later reappears with Certification Authorities, KRBTGT, privileged provisioning systems, directory replication authority, and cloud identity control planes.

Identity systems contain several places where compromise changes from stealing trust to manufacturing it.

Federation signing infrastructure is one of them.
