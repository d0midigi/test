# 5.9.2 Trust Agreements Are Security Architecture

Federation is sometimes treated technically as the exchange of metadata, certificates, endpoints, claims, and tokens.

Those components are important.

The trust relationship itself begins before the protocol exchange.

The parties must define which identities can participate, which attributes may be exchanged, what assurance is required, how changes are communicated, how signing material is managed, and how the relationship is terminated or suspended.

The current NIST federation model explicitly incorporates trust agreements at higher Federation Assurance Levels because cryptographic token validation alone does not define the complete relationship between independently administered parties. (\[6])

This is especially significant for mission-partner federation.

An external partner may change its identity architecture.

A certificate may rotate.

An identity provider may be compromised.

An attribute definition may change.

A mission may terminate.

The relying environment needs a process for responding to those changes without rediscovering the federation's ownership during the incident.

The trust agreement is therefore part of security operations, not merely procurement or interagency administration.
