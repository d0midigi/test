# 5.9.4 Federation Governance Must Include Failure

A federation architecture is incomplete if it describes only how trust is established.

The design must also account for how trust ends.

If one partner reports credential compromise, can the affected identity be blocked locally without suspending every partner user?

If the identity provider itself is compromised, can the relying organization rapidly stop accepting its assertions?

If signing keys are replaced, can relying parties update trust safely without unnecessary outage?

If a mission relationship ends, can associated access be identified and removed reliably?

These are lifecycle questions, but federation magnifies them because responsibility is distributed.

The organization relying on the identity may not control the system that issued it.

The organization operating the identity provider may not know every resource that consumes its assertions.

Effective federation governance therefore requires both sides to understand the dependency they have created.

Federation succeeds operationally because trust can cross an administrative boundary.

Federation succeeds securely only when that boundary remains governable.
