# 5.5.2 Common Access Card

The Common Access Card (CAC) performs a comparable credentialing role within the Department of Defense, although the CAC exists within the DoD personnel, credentialing, PKI, and access ecosystem rather than simply being another name for a civilian agency PIV card.

From the perspective of Active Directory authentication, the important architectural relationship is similar: the credential can contain certificates and cryptographic keys used to establish authenticated identity, and DoD relying systems can use those credentials for logical access. Federal FICAM guidance explicitly identifies both PIV and CAC smart cards as examples of agency-issued credentials containing cryptographic key pairs for asserting identity. ([1](https://www.idmanagement.gov/arch/))

The existence of a common DoD credential provides substantial advantages for identity interoperability. A user does not need a completely unrelated credential for every system merely because the resource belongs to another DoD organization. Common credential infrastructure allows systems to rely on standardized cryptographic identity mechanisms while retaining local control over authorization.

That second half is essential.

The CAC establishes a credential that can participate in authentication.

It does not create universal DoD authorization.

Possession of a valid CAC does not entitle the holder to arbitrary NIPRNet, SIPRNet, application, administrative, or mission-resource access. Each relying environment still determines whether the authenticated identity is authorized and at what level.

This separation allows identity to be reusable without making authority universal.

The distinction also explains why revocation and lifecycle management matter at a department scale. A reusable enterprise credential becomes operationally valuable precisely because many systems are willing to trust it. The same characteristic increases the consequences of a credential that should no longer be accepted but remains usable.

Credential lifecycle therefore has to keep pace with identity lifecycle.
