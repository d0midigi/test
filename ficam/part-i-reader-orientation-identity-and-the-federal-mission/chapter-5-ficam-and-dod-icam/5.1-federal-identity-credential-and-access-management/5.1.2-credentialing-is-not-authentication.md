# 5.1.2 Credentialing Is Not Authentication

Credentialing and authentication are also routinely collapsed into one concept.

They should remain distinct.

Credentialing establishes and manages a credential associated with an identity. Authentication is the event in which a claimant demonstrates control of an authenticator associated with that identity or subscriber account.

A PIV or CAC provides a useful example.

The credential must first be issued through an authorized process and associated with the correct person. Certificates and cryptographic keys are provisioned to the credential under defined requirements. Only later does a user present the credential during an authentication event.

The authentication event can succeed perfectly while the broader credential lifecycle is already unhealthy.

A credential may no longer be appropriate because the person's status changed.

A certificate may remain technically valid while the account it represents should no longer receive access.

An alternate authenticator may have been improperly registered to the same identity.

The authenticator may be strong while the enrollment or recovery process is weak.

Identity security therefore cannot begin at the logon screen.

The security of authentication depends partly on what happened before authentication occurred.
