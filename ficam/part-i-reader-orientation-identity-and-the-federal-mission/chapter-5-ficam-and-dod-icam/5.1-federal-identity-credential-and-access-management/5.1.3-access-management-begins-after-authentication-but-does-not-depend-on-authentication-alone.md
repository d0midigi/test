# 5.1.3 Access Management Begins After Authentication but Does Not Depend on Authentication Alone

Authentication establishes that a claimant is accepted as a particular subscriber or principal.

Access management decides what that principal may do.

In simple environments, the distinction may appear to be little more than username plus group membership.

Federal identity environments can be substantially richer.

Authorization may depend on organizational affiliation, position, role, account type, mission assignment, device state, application context, information sensitivity, or another trusted attribute in addition to the authenticated identity.

This is where FICAM intersects directly with the authorization architecture explored later in this book.

A directory group is one way to represent entitlement.

It is not the only way.

An Active Directory Access Control Entry may assign authority directly.

A federation claim may carry an attribute consumed by an application.

A cloud application may evaluate role assignments and Conditional Access context.

A policy engine may make an Attribute-Based Access Control decision from several authoritative inputs.

The security problem therefore extends beyond whether the account authenticated successfully.

The enterprise must preserve the integrity of the data from which authorization is derived.
