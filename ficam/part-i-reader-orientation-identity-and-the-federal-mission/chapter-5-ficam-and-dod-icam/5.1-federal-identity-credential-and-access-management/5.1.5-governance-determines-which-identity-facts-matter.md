# 5.1.5 Governance Determines Which Identity Facts Matter

The technical systems involved in ICAM can produce enormous numbers of identity attributes.

Not all of them should be authoritative.

Not all of them should influence access.

A user's display name may be useful for presentation but inappropriate for security decisions. An organizational field may be descriptive in one system and authoritative in another. A locally edited directory attribute may look identical to an attribute populated from an enterprise personnel system while possessing completely different evidentiary value.

Governance determines which source is authoritative for which fact.

That decision becomes a security boundary.

If an access policy depends on an attribute, the security of the policy depends on who can change that attribute and where the value originates.

This is particularly important for Attribute-Based Access Control (ABAC).

ABAC is often described as more dynamic or context-aware than traditional group-based authorization. That flexibility is real, but it increases dependence on attribute integrity.

A sophisticated policy engine supplied with untrustworthy data can make bad decisions faster and more consistently than a simple one.

FICAM governance therefore matters at the data layer, not merely at the policy-document layer.
