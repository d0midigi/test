# 5.8.1 Entitlement Should Express a Defensible Relationship

Every access grant represents a relationship between a principal and a resource.

The principal has some recognized identity.

The resource performs some function.

A policy or mission requirement explains why the relationship should exist.

Access governance maintains that connection over time.

The difficulty is that technical systems are better at recording the entitlement than recording the reason.

Active Directory can show that a user belongs to `Server-Admins-West`.

It may not explain why.

The application can show that a service principal has an application permission.

It may not identify which project originally required it.

A DACL can show that a group has control over an Organizational Unit.

It may not identify the administrative design that justified the delegation.

When rationale disappears, the entitlement becomes difficult to challenge.

This is how excessive access becomes institutionalized.

Nobody wants to remove the permission because nobody is certain what will fail.

Good access governance preserves enough ownership and purpose that entitlement can be reevaluated without reconstructing years of organizational history from audit logs and tribal knowledge.
