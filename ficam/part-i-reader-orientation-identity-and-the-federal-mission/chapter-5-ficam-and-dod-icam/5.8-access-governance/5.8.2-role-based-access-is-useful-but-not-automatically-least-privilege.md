# 5.8.2 Role-Based Access Is Useful but Not Automatically Least Privilege

Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) can make entitlement substantially more manageable by assigning permissions to defined roles rather than directly to every individual.

Active Directory groups are commonly used to implement portions of that model.

But a group is not automatically a well-engineered role.

A group can contain the wrong users.

A group can possess excessive permissions.

Groups can be nested in ways that obscure effective membership.

A role can outlive the business function it was designed to represent.

The presence of RBAC terminology therefore does not establish least privilege.

The design still has to be evaluated.

A properly governed role should correspond to a recognizable function, possess access appropriate to that function, have an identifiable owner, and support periodic review.

When one role gradually absorbs unrelated permissions because adding them is operationally convenient, it becomes a privilege aggregator rather than a security boundary.

The same caution applies to Attribute-Based Access Control.

ABAC can create precise policy decisions using rich context, but its security depends on the integrity and governance of the attributes driving the decision.

Access-control models provide mechanisms.

Governance determines whether those mechanisms remain aligned with actual mission need.
