# 5.8.4 Separation of Duties Must Survive Indirect Control

Separation of Duties (SoD) is intended to prevent one principal or role from exercising combinations of authority that should require independent actors.

Identity systems can undermine that principle indirectly.

Two administrative functions may be assigned to separate accounts while one account has permission to reset the other's password.

Two teams may appear administratively separate while one management platform controls the workstations used by both.

Certificate administration may be separated from directory administration while a directory administrator can modify the groups governing certificate enrollment.

Backup administration may be considered independent from Domain Admin while backup operators can restore or manipulate Domain Controller state.

Formal role separation therefore has to be compared with effective technical control.

If one role can manufacture, replace, impersonate, or remotely control the other, the separation may exist on paper while disappearing operationally.

This principle becomes central when the book reaches the trusted core and Tier 0 architecture.
