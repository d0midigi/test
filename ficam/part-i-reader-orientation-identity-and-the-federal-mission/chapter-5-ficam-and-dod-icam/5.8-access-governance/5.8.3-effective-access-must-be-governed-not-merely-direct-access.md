# 5.8.3 Effective Access Must Be Governed, Not Merely Direct Access

One of the most important differences between identity administration and identity security is the treatment of indirect authority.

An access review may show that a user is not a Domain Admin.

That is useful information.

It is not proof that the user cannot reach Domain Admin-equivalent authority.

The user may control a group that controls another group.

The user may possess authority over an account that is privileged.

The user may control a Group Policy Object applied to privileged systems.

The user may administer a management platform capable of deploying software to Domain Controllers.

The user may be able to enroll a certificate representing a privileged identity.

Effective access includes those paths.

This is why attack-path analysis belongs within access governance rather than exclusively within penetration testing.

The offensive tool may expose the relationship.

The governance failure existed before the test.

A mature access-governance program therefore needs some means of evaluating effective control, especially around Tier 0 and other high-value systems.

Otherwise, recertification can produce technically accurate statements that miss the authority that matters most.
