# 5.13.6 FICAM Provides the Identity Discipline Zero Trust Requires

The relationship between FICAM and Zero Trust becomes clearest when viewed operationally.

Zero Trust requires high-quality identity signals.

FICAM provides governance for establishing and maintaining them.

Zero Trust requires strong authentication appropriate to risk.

FICAM and the federal digital identity model provide the identity-proofing, credentialing, authenticator, and federation disciplines supporting that assurance.

Zero Trust requires precise authorization.

FICAM provides lifecycle, entitlement, federation, and attribute-governance concepts through which that authorization can be managed.

Zero Trust requires timely revocation.

FICAM treats deactivation, credential revocation, and identity lifecycle as enterprise processes rather than isolated account operations.

The two therefore meet in the access decision.

FICAM establishes what the enterprise believes about the identity.

Zero Trust determines how much of that belief is sufficient for a particular access request under the conditions that exist at that moment.

Neither is complete without defensible technical implementation.

And that is where the book moves next.

The governance and identity architecture established in Chapters 3 through 5 describe what the federal identity trust system is trying to achieve. The remainder of Part II begins examining the infrastructure that actually carries those decisions across the enterprise: networks, Domain Name System, service discovery, transport protocols, network-access authentication, directory architecture, replication, authorization, Kerberos, credentials, policy, certificates, federation, and hybrid identity.

The policy layer has established the intended trust.

The next layer shows how that trust moves across the wire.
