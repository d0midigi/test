# 5.13.3 Device Identity Becomes Part of Access Trust

Traditional Active Directory authorization focuses heavily on user and group identity.

Zero Trust architectures increasingly incorporate device context.

This expands the identity trust system.

The enterprise may need confidence that the device is known, managed, appropriately configured, cryptographically represented, compliant with security requirements, and not presenting risk conditions incompatible with the requested resource.

Device identity therefore becomes more than an inventory record.

It becomes an input to authorization.

This creates another authoritative-data problem.

Which platform determines whether the device is compliant?

How quickly is that state updated?

What happens if the management platform is compromised?

Can the device identity be duplicated?

Can a stale device remain trusted after it leaves management?

Once device state becomes authorization state, management of the device identity becomes part of the access-control plane.

This is the same pattern repeatedly observed throughout ICAM: data becomes security-critical when another system begins trusting it.
