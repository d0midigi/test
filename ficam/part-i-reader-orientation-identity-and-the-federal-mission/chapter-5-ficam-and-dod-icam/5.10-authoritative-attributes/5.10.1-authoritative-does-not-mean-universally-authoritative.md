# 5.10.1 Authoritative Does Not Mean Universally Authoritative

An enterprise rarely has one system that is authoritative for every fact about an identity.

A personnel system may be authoritative for employment status.

A directory may be authoritative for a locally assigned username.

An asset-management or endpoint-management system may be authoritative for device ownership or compliance state.

A privileged-access platform may be authoritative for whether a particular administrative role is currently activated.

An application may remain authoritative for a specialized entitlement that has meaning nowhere else.

The resulting identity record is therefore often assembled from several authorities.

This is why the phrase _single source of truth_ requires careful use in identity engineering.

There may be a single authoritative source for a specific attribute or identity relationship.

There is not necessarily one database from which every meaningful identity fact originates.

Trying to force all authority into one repository can actually obscure provenance. The receiving directory begins to look authoritative because all of the values are stored there, even though the organization may have lost visibility into where individual values originated and who is permitted to change them.

A stronger model preserves provenance.

The system consuming an attribute should be able to determine, either directly or through governed architecture, why that attribute is considered trustworthy and which authority owns its lifecycle.
