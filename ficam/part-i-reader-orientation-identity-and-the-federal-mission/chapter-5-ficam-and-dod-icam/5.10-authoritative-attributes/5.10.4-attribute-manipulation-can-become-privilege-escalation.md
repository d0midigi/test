# 5.10.4 Attribute Manipulation Can Become Privilege Escalation

Traditional privilege escalation often produces visible changes: membership in a privileged group, possession of a more powerful credential, elevation to a local administrator, or control of a more privileged system.

Attribute-driven authorization can produce escalation less visibly.

An attacker may never modify the protected resource.

Instead, the attacker changes the identity facts that cause the protected resource to authorize access.

Suppose an application grants administrative privileges based on an enterprise role attribute. The attacker gains write authority over that attribute but has no administrative permission inside the application.

The application continues enforcing its policy exactly as configured.

The problem is that the input to the policy has been corrupted.

This type of attack is conceptually similar to manipulating group membership, but modern ICAM architectures can make the relationship much less obvious because the attribute may pass through several systems before affecting access.

The attack path may begin in a personnel integration, move through an identity-governance platform, synchronize into a directory, become a federation claim, and finally be interpreted by an application.

None of those systems needs to be individually compromised at the software level.

Control over the right upstream value may be enough.

This is why attribute security belongs within attack-path analysis.

The graph of authority includes data provenance.
