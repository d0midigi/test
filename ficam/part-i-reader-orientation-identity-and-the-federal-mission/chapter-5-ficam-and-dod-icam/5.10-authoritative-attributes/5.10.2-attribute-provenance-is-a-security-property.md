# 5.10.2 Attribute Provenance Is a Security Property

Provenance describes where identity information came from and how it arrived at the point where it is being used.

This becomes critical when the same attribute can be written through several mechanisms.

Consider an attribute populated from an authoritative enterprise source through automated provisioning. If an ordinary delegated administrator can also edit the same attribute manually in Active Directory, the attribute's downstream consumer may be unable to distinguish authoritative state from a locally introduced change.

The synchronization job may be secure.

The source may be secure.

The authorization system may be secure.

The writable copy in the middle can still undermine the entire chain.

This is an identity-integrity problem.

A mature architecture therefore considers not only who can read an attribute, but who can write it, what system is expected to write it, whether local changes are allowed, how conflicting values are reconciled, and what happens when synchronization fails.

These questions become especially important for attributes that affect authorization.

A telephone number may tolerate a temporary discrepancy.

A role indicator that grants access to a mission application may not.

The security importance of an attribute is determined partly by what depends on it.
