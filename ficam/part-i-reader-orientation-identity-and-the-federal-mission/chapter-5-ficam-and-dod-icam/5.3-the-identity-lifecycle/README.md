# 5.3 The Identity Lifecycle

The identity lifecycle is the connective tissue between governance and technical implementation.

FICAM's current lifecycle guidance treats identity management as a process extending through creation, proofing, vetting, provisioning, aggregation, maintenance, and eventual deactivation. The DoD ICAM Reference Design similarly addresses identity registration, credentialing, attribute management, access, and deactivation across enterprise, Component, partner, and local implementations. ([2](https://www.idmanagement.gov/playbooks/ilm/))

The lifecycle matters to security because identity risk changes over time.

A newly created account may begin with appropriate access.

Months later, the person's role changes.

The account accumulates another group.

A temporary entitlement becomes permanent.

A second authenticator is registered.

The original sponsor leaves.

The user transfers organizations.

A service account becomes critical infrastructure.

The digital identity is still functioning.

Its relationship to the real-world authority that originally justified it may no longer be accurate.

Identity security therefore cannot be evaluated only at creation.

Trust has to be maintained.
