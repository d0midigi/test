# 5.3.6 Authentication

Authentication is the point at which a claimant proves control of one or more authenticators bound to the subscriber account.

The current NIST SP 800-63B-4 defines three Authenticator Assurance Levels and establishes progressively stronger requirements for the authentication ceremony, cryptography, resistance to replay and phishing, authenticator protection, reauthentication, and related properties. ([4](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/63/b/4/final))

Authentication is critical, but it occupies only one position in the lifecycle.

The subject was established before authentication.

The credential was bound before authentication.

Authorization follows authentication.

Monitoring continues after authentication.

Revocation may later invalidate the authenticator.

This explains why an authentication-centric security program can remain weak despite excellent multifactor authentication.

If lifecycle governance is poor, the organization may strongly authenticate stale identities.

If authorization is poor, it may strongly authenticate overprivileged identities.

If credential recovery is weak, an attacker may bypass the strongest normal authenticator by manipulating the process used to replace it.

Authentication strength is essential.

It is not a substitute for identity integrity.
