# 5.3.4 Credential Issuance

Credential issuance provides the subject with an approved mechanism through which identity can later be authenticated.

In federal workforce environments, this may include PIV or CAC credentials and associated certificates. Other contexts may use derived credentials, cryptographic authenticators, passwords, FIDO authenticators, or other mechanisms appropriate to the required authentication assurance.

Issuance is a security boundary because the credential becomes a portable representation of trust.

Once issued, the credential may be presented to systems far removed from the original enrollment process.

The relying system often has no visibility into the entire ceremony that created it.

It sees only the resulting credential and the trust chain supporting it.

This is why issuance integrity matters.

A credential should not merely be technically valid.

It should be valid because the correct authority issued it to the correct identity under the correct assurance process.
