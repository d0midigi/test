# 5.3.9 Revocation and Deprovisioning

Revocation removes trust from a credential or authentication mechanism.

Deprovisioning removes or disables identity representations and access that are no longer authorized.

The two operations overlap but are not the same.

A certificate can be revoked while the account remains active.

An account can be disabled while a cloud session remains valid.

A password can be changed while a stolen certificate continues to authenticate.

A user can be removed from Active Directory while a separate federated or application-specific identity remains.

This distinction becomes extremely important during incident response.

Containment has to identify every form of authority associated with the compromised identity.

Changing the password may be necessary and completely insufficient.

The same principle applies to normal offboarding.

A mature identity lifecycle does not assume that disabling one account ends the relationship.

It follows the identity through each system that learned to trust it.
