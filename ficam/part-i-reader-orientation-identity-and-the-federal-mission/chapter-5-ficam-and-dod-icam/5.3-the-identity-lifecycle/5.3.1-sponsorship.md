# 5.3.1 Sponsorship

Sponsorship establishes why an identity should exist within the enterprise.

This is especially important for identities whose presence is not established automatically through a central workforce process. Contractors, mission partners, temporary personnel, external collaborators, certain service identities, and specialized local identities may require an accountable party to justify their creation and continued presence.

The sponsor is not merely the person who submits the account request.

Sponsorship creates an ownership relationship.

Someone must be able to explain why the identity exists, which mission or business function it supports, what authority it requires, and when that authority should be reviewed or removed.

This becomes crucial during recertification.

A stale identity with an active sponsor can be questioned.

A stale identity with no known owner is much harder to govern because the organization has lost the context necessary to distinguish necessary access from historical residue.

The offensive significance is obvious.

Orphaned identity is attractive identity.

An account whose purpose is no longer understood is less likely to receive scrutiny when its behavior changes.

A service account whose owner is unknown is less likely to have its credential rotated.

A partner account whose mission ended may remain active simply because no one is responsible for removing it.

Sponsorship is therefore one of the earliest security controls in the identity lifecycle.
