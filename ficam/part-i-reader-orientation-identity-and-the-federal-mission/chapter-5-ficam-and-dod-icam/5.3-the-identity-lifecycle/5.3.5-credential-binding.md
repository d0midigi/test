# 5.3.5 Credential Binding

Credential binding associates an authenticator with the subscriber identity that is allowed to use it.

This step is sometimes underestimated because the credential and identity often appear together operationally.

From a security perspective, however, the binding is the entire point.

A cryptographic token proves control of a private key.

It does not inherently prove which person that key should represent.

The identity system supplies that relationship.

The same issue appears with modern authenticators.

A FIDO credential may be highly resistant to phishing, yet registering an attacker's authenticator to a victim's account would still result in account compromise.

The problem would not be weakness in FIDO.

It would be unauthorized binding.

Authenticator enrollment, replacement, recovery, and re-binding therefore deserve the same attention as the authentication protocol itself.

Many account-takeover paths move toward recovery or registration precisely because the normal authenticator is difficult to defeat.
