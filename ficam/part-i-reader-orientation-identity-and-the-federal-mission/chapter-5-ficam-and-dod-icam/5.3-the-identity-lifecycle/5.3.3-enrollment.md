# 5.3.3 Enrollment

Enrollment converts the successfully proofed subject into a maintained digital identity relationship.

This is where evidence and verified attributes become associated with a subscriber account or corresponding enterprise identity record and where authenticators can subsequently be bound under the applicable process. NIST SP 800-63A-4 treats proofing and enrollment as a connected process and requires successful proofing to result in subscriber-account state capable of supporting later authenticator binding and lifecycle maintenance. ([6](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-63a-4.pdf))

From an Active Directory perspective, enrollment is conceptually upstream from simply creating `CN=Jane Smith,OU=Users,...`.

The directory object should represent identity state that has already been established through an approved process.

This distinction becomes important when multiple accounts represent one person.

A privileged administrative account, an ordinary workforce account, a cloud account, an enclave-specific identity, and an application account may all correspond to the same underlying person while serving different security functions.

The enterprise needs a way to preserve that relationship without treating each account as a separate human identity.

Enrollment establishes the authoritative identity relationship.

Provisioning later creates the technical representations required by individual systems.
