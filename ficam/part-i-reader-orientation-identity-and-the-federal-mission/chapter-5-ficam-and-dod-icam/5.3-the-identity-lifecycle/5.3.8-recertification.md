# 5.3.8 Recertification

Identity state decays unless it is periodically revalidated.

People change roles.

Contracts expire.

Mission assignments end.

Temporary access becomes forgotten access.

Administrative privileges accumulate.

Service identities acquire new dependencies.

Applications are retired without removing associated accounts.

Recertification exists to challenge that accumulated state.

The purpose is not to ask managers whether a familiar-looking spreadsheet still looks acceptable.

Effective recertification should re-establish the business or mission basis for access and identify authority that no longer has a defensible owner or requirement.

For privileged identities, that review should account for effective control rather than group membership alone.

An account removed from a privileged group may still retain delegated rights capable of recreating the same authority.

A service identity may appear unprivileged while running on infrastructure that gives it access to privileged credentials.

A cloud service principal may possess application permissions with no human-readable equivalent to a traditional administrator group.

Recertification must therefore mature alongside the technologies being governed.
