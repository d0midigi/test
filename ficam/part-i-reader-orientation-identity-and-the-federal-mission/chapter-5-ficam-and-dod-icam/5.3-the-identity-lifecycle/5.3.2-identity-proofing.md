# 5.3.2 Identity Proofing

Identity proofing establishes confidence that the subject being enrolled corresponds to the claimed real-world identity.

The current NIST SP 800-63A-4 separates proofing into activities involving evidence collection, validation, attribute validation, and verification and defines three Identity Assurance Levels with progressively stronger requirements. The 2025 revision also strengthens treatment of fraud, forged evidence, biometric processes, digital evidence, remote proofing, and proofing threats. ([3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/63/a/4/final))

This process is fundamentally different from account authentication.

Authentication asks whether the person presenting an authenticator now is the subscriber associated with the account.

Identity proofing asks whether the subscriber account was associated with the correct person in the first place.

That distinction is foundational.

Strong authentication cannot correct a proofing failure.

If the wrong person is successfully enrolled and bound to a high-assurance authenticator, the authenticator may provide extremely strong proof that the wrong person controls the credential.

The cryptography has not failed.

The identity binding has.

For federal identity systems, proofing therefore establishes the trust from which later authentication inherits meaning.
