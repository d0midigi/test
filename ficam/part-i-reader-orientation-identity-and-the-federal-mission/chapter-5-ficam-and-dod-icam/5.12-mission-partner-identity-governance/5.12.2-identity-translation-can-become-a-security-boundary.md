# 5.12.2 Identity Translation Can Become a Security Boundary

Mission-partner access often requires translating externally understood identity information into locally meaningful identity state.

An external subject identifier may need to be correlated with a local guest object.

A partner attribute may be mapped to a local role.

A certificate subject may be mapped to a directory account.

A federation claim may be transformed before reaching an application.

These translation points deserve the same scrutiny as authentication mechanisms.

An attacker may not need to defeat the partner's authenticator if the mapping process can be manipulated.

The external identity can be completely legitimate while the local representation is wrong.

This is especially dangerous when identity mapping is based on mutable or weakly governed values.

A display name is a poor security identifier.

An email-style name may change.

A locally editable attribute may not preserve authoritative provenance.

A mapping mechanism designed for administrative convenience can inadvertently bind one external identity to another local principal.

Identity translation therefore belongs inside the trust boundary.

The fact that two systems agree on a string does not prove that they agree on the same identity.
