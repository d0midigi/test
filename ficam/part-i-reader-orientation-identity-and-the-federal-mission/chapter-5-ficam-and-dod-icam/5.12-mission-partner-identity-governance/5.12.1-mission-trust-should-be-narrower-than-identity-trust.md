# 5.12.1 Mission Trust Should Be Narrower Than Identity Trust

A mission partner may be a trusted identity source.

That does not mean every identity from that partner should receive access.

Likewise, acceptance of one identity from a partner should not imply acceptance of every attribute, authentication context, or authorization assertion the partner can provide.

Trust should be scoped.

This is the difference between recognizing another organization's identity authority and delegating local resource governance to it.

The relying environment may accept that a partner correctly identifies and authenticates a person while still requiring local sponsorship, mission assignment, entitlement approval, or resource-owner authorization before access is granted.

This layered model is more resilient because compromise or administrative error at one layer does not automatically create unrestricted downstream authority.

The external organization establishes who the subject is.

The mission establishes why the subject needs access.

The resource owner establishes what access is appropriate.

The relying environment enforces the resulting authorization.

These layers should reinforce one another rather than collapse into a single external claim.
