# 5.12.4 Partner Trust Requires an Incident Path

Federation is often engineered carefully for normal operations and much less carefully for crisis conditions.

The weakness appears when one side reports compromise.

If one partner user's credential is compromised, the local environment should be able to contain that identity without unnecessarily disabling every user from the same partner.

If the partner's identity provider is compromised, the response problem becomes broader. The relying organization may have to distrust assertions that remain cryptographically valid because the authority producing them can no longer be considered trustworthy.

If a signing key is compromised, ordinary account disablement may be inadequate.

If identity attributes are corrupted upstream, the relying system may receive incorrect authorization data from an otherwise functioning federation service.

The incident-response process must therefore distinguish subject compromise from issuer compromise.

Those are different security states.

A compromised subject requires local access containment and coordinated credential remediation.

A compromised identity authority may require suspension or reconstruction of the trust relationship itself.

Mission-partner governance should define those paths before either organization needs them.
