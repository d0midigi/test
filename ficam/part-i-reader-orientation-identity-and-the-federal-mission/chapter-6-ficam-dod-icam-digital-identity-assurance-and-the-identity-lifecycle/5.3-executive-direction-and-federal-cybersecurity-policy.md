# 5.3 Executive Direction and Federal Cybersecurity Policy

## 5.3 Executive Direction and Federal Cybersecurity Policy

### 5.3.1 Executive Orders as Cybersecurity Catalysts

{% hint style="info" %}
Executive Orders can accelerate federal cybersecurity modernization by directing agencies toward stronger authentication, Zero Trust architectures, software-supply-chain controls, cloud modernization, logging, and improved incident-response practices.
{% endhint %}

### 5.3.2 Office of Management and Budget

{% hint style="info" %}
The Office of Management and Budget (OMB) translates executive priorities into federal agency requirements, implementation deadlines, reporting expectations, and cybersecurity policy.
{% endhint %}

### 5.3.3 OMB and Federal Identity Modernization

{% hint style="info" %}
OMB policy has played a significant role in strengthening Personal Identity Verification, phishing-resistant authentication, Zero Trust adoption, and identity-centered security across federal agencies.
{% endhint %}

### 5.3.4 Homeland Security Presidential Directive 12

{% hint style="info" %}
Homeland Security Presidential Directive 12 (HSPD-12) established a government-wide policy for secure and reliable identification issued by the federal government.
{% endhint %}

### 5.3.5 Federal Credentialing as an Enterprise Requirement

{% hint style="info" %}
HSPD-12 helped move federal credentialing away from agency-specific identity cards toward interoperable, standards-based credentials tied to stronger identity proofing and authentication.
{% endhint %}
