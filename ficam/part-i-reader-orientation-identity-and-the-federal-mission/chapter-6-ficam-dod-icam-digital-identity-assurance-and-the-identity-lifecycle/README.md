# Chapter 6 - FICAM, DoD ICAM, Digital Identity Assurance, and the Identity Lifecycle

### Abstract

Chapter 6 develops the identity-governance architecture underlying federal and Department of Defense access decisions. It distinguishes identities from accounts, credentials from authentication, and authentication from authorization while examining the complete lifecycle of identity establishment, sponsorship, proofing, enrollment, provisioning, credential issuance, authentication, authorization, recertification, suspension, revocation, and deprovisioning. Identity Assurance Levels, Authenticator Assurance Levels, and Federation Assurance Levels are examined alongside PIV, CAC, EDIPI, biometrics, derived credentials, authoritative sources, identity correlation, and persistent identifiers.

Chapter 6 addresses both Person Entities and Non-Person Entities (NPE), including devices, service accounts, workloads, applications, and automation. Access governance models such as RBAC, ABAC, DAC, MAC, Conditional Access, least privilege, separation of duties, and privileged identity management are evaluated as engineering mechanisms rather than abstract policy concepts. The chapter also examines federation, mission-partner identity, sponsorship, identity debt, Zero Trust integration, and identity-governance telemetry, establishing the lifecycle discipline required to maintain trustworthy access over time.
