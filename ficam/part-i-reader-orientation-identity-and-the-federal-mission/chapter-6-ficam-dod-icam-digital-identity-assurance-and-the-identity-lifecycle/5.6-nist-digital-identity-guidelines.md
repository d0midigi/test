# 5.6 NIST Digital Identity Guidelines

## 5.6 NIST Digital Identity Guidelines

### 5.6.1 NIST Special Publication 800-63

{% hint style="info" %}
NIST Special Publication 800-63 provides the federal digital-identity framework for identity proofing, authentication, authenticator lifecycle management, and federation.
{% endhint %}

### 5.6.2 Identity Assurance Levels

{% hint style="info" %}
Identity Assurance Levels (IALs) describe the confidence associated with identity proofing and the relationship between a digital identity and a real-world subject.
{% endhint %}

### 5.6.3 Authenticator Assurance Levels

{% hint style="info" %}
Authenticator Assurance Levels (AALs) define increasing levels of confidence in the authentication mechanisms used to access systems.
{% endhint %}

### 5.6.4 Federation Assurance Levels

{% hint style="info" %}
Federation Assurance Levels (FALs) describe security requirements for federation assertions and the protection of federated authentication exchanges.
{% endhint %}

### 5.6.5 Assurance Must Be Preserved End to End

{% hint style="info" %}
A strong authenticator does not preserve high assurance when recovery processes, federation mappings, identity attributes, session handling, or downstream authorization introduce weaker trust.
{% endhint %}

### 5.6.6 Identity Proofing Is Part of Cybersecurity Architecture

{% hint style="info" %}
The security of an account begins before its creation when the enterprise decides who or what the identity represents and how that relationship is established.
{% endhint %}
