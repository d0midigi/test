# 5.9 Department of Defense ICAM Governance

## 5.9 Department of Defense ICAM Governance

### 5.9.1 DoD ICAM as an Enterprise Capability

{% hint style="info" %}
Department of Defense Identity, Credential, and Access Management (DoD ICAM) supports enterprise identity across military departments, combatant commands, defense agencies, mission partners, cloud environments, and shared services.
{% endhint %}

### 5.9.2 Enterprise ICAM Does Not Eliminate Local Engineering

{% hint style="info" %}
Central services do not remove the requirement for commands and system owners to correctly engineer local Active Directory, application authorization, privileged access, certificate usage, and mission-specific identity dependencies.
{% endhint %}

### 5.9.3 DoD ICAM Strategy

{% hint style="info" %}
The DoD ICAM Strategy establishes enterprise direction for identity data, credentialing, authentication, authorization, federation, governance, and modernization.
{% endhint %}

### 5.9.4 DoD Enterprise ICAM Reference Design

{% hint style="info" %}
The DoD Enterprise ICAM Reference Design translates strategic direction into architectural concepts that can guide implementation across interconnected mission environments.
{% endhint %}

### 5.9.5 DoD ICAM Federation

{% hint style="info" %}
Federation supports mission-partner and cross-enterprise identity while preserving administrative autonomy among participating environments.
{% endhint %}

### 5.9.6 Person Entities and Non-Person Entities

{% hint style="info" %}
DoD ICAM must govern both human identities and Non-Person Entities (NPEs), including devices, workloads, applications, services, and automation.
{% endhint %}

### 5.9.7 Disconnected and Tactical ICAM

{% hint style="info" %}
Military identity architecture must account for environments where enterprise connectivity is degraded, intermittent, denied, or intentionally unavailable.
{% endhint %}
