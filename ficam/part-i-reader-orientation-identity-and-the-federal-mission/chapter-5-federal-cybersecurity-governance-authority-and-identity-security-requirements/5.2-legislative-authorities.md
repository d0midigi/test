# 5.2 Legislative Authorities

## 5.2 Legislative Authorities

### 5.2.1 Federal Information Security Modernization Act

{% hint style="info" %}
The Federal Information Security Modernization Act (FISMA) establishes foundational federal information-security responsibilities, including risk-based protection, security programs, assessment, continuous monitoring, and reporting.
{% endhint %}

### 5.2.2 FISMA and Identity Security

{% hint style="info" %}
Identity systems fall within FISMA risk-management obligations because failures in authentication, authorization, privileged access, credentials, and trust relationships can compromise the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of federal information systems.
{% endhint %}

### 5.2.3 National Security System Authorities

{% hint style="info" %}
National Security Systems (NSS) may operate under authorities and risk considerations that differ from ordinary civilian federal information systems.
{% endhint %}

### 5.2.4 National-Security Mission Requirements

{% hint style="info" %}
Classified processing, intelligence missions, command-and-control functions, military operations, weapons-supporting systems, and related environments can introduce additional technical and operational expectations for identity infrastructure.
{% endhint %}

### 5.2.5 Privacy and Information-Protection Requirements

{% hint style="info" %}
Identity systems commonly store or process Personally Identifiable Information (PII), personnel information, credential data, authentication records, and other sensitive information whose protection extends beyond access-control mechanics alone.
{% endhint %}

### 5.2.6 Controlled Unclassified Information

{% hint style="info" %}
Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) introduces safeguarding requirements that can affect identity proofing, access control, logging, authentication, remote access, contractor connectivity, and information sharing.
{% endhint %}
