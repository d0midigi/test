# 5.5 National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Publications

## 5.5 National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Publications

### 5.5.1 The Role of NIST

{% hint style="info" %}
The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) provides standards, guidelines, architectures, controls, and technical publications that form much of the federal cybersecurity and identity-security foundation.
{% endhint %}

### 5.5.2 NIST Special Publication 800-37

{% hint style="info" %}
NIST Special Publication 800-37 establishes the Risk Management Framework (RMF) and defines the lifecycle through which systems are prepared, categorized, controlled, assessed, authorized, and continuously monitored.
{% endhint %}

### 5.5.3 NIST Special Publication 800-53

{% hint style="info" %}
NIST Special Publication 800-53 provides the security and privacy control catalog used throughout federal risk management.
{% endhint %}

### 5.5.4 Identity-Relevant NIST SP 800-53 Control Families

{% hint style="info" %}
Identity infrastructure intersects heavily with Access Control (AC), Identification and Authentication (IA), Audit and Accountability (AU), Configuration Management (CM), Contingency Planning (CP), System and Communications Protection (SC), System and Information Integrity (SI), and related control families.
{% endhint %}

### 5.5.5 NIST Special Publication 800-53A

{% hint style="info" %}
NIST Special Publication 800-53A provides assessment procedures for determining whether controls are implemented correctly, operating as intended, and producing the desired outcome.
{% endhint %}

### 5.5.6 Technical Testing Must Support Control Assessment

{% hint style="info" %}
Directory permissions, Kerberos settings, authentication protocols, privileged access, certificate templates, federation configuration, and cloud identity policy may all require technical examination before a control can be considered meaningfully assessed.
{% endhint %}
