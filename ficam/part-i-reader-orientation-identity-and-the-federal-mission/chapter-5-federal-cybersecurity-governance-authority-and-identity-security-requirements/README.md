# Chapter 5 - Federal Cybersecurity Governance, Authority, and Identity Security Requirements

### Abstract

Chapter 5 takes the readers through how federal cybersecurity requirements move from law, executive direction, standards, policy, and technical guidance into enforceable identity-security configuration. It examines FISMA, HSPD-12, FIPS 199, FIPS 200, FIPS 201, FIPS 140, NIST Special Publications 800-37, 800-53, 800-53A, 800-63, and 800-207, along with FICAM, DoD ICAM governance, Department of Defense issuances, DISA STIGs and SRGs, vulnerability directives, CISA responsibilities, FedRAMP, DoD cloud requirements, and Defense Industrial Base considerations.

Additionally, this chapter distinguishes governing authority from technical implementation and shows how requirements ultimately become Group Policy, directory permissions, certificate configuration, Conditional Access, authentication controls, firewall rules, and operational evidence. It also examines the difference between control implementation and actual security outcomes, emphasizing traceability, defensibility, evidence quality, exceptions, and effective authority. Governance is presented not as paperwork but as a mechanism for identifying assumptions, measuring technical state, prioritizing attack-path remediation, and connecting cyber risk to accountable mission decisions.
