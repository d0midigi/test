# Chapter 62 - Identity Supply Chain, Third-Party Integrations, and Administrative Dependency Security

### Abstract

Federal and DoD identity environments increasingly depend on software vendors, managed services, identity brokers, security platforms, administrative agents, cloud applications, remote-management tools, synchronization services, update mechanisms, and third-party integrations that can create or exercise substantial identity authority. This chapter examines the identity supply chain as an extension of the enterprise trust graph. Offensive analysis focuses on how adversaries exploit privileged integrations, compromised software updates, vendor credentials, OAuth permissions, service principals, remote support platforms, management agents, secrets, signing infrastructure, and inherited administrative relationships to bypass otherwise hardened identity boundaries. Defensive analysis emphasizes dependency discovery, effective-authority mapping, least-privilege integration, credential isolation, software provenance, signing validation, tenant restrictions, vendor-access control, telemetry, kill switches, revocation, and recovery planning. The chapter establishes that an identity architecture cannot be considered secure if external software, services, or administrative dependencies can silently recreate the authority that internal controls were designed to constrain.
