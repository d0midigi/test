# NEW Chapter 60 - Identity Threat Intelligence, Campaign Analysis, and Adversary Emulation

### Abstract

Identity threat intelligence connects observed adversary behavior outside the enterprise to the specific identities, credentials, protocols, trust relationships, and control planes that exist inside a federal or DoD environment. This chapter examines the collection, analysis, correlation, and operational use of identity-centric threat intelligence across Active Directory, Microsoft Entra ID, Public Key Infrastructure, cloud services, applications, non-person entities, federation, and privileged infrastructure. Offensive analysis focuses on how adversaries sequence reconnaissance, credential access, privilege escalation, persistence, lateral movement, defense evasion, and control-plane compromise across campaigns. Defensive analysis emphasizes translating external reporting into local attack-path hypotheses, detection opportunities, hunt questions, exposure prioritization, adversary-emulation scenarios, and engineering changes. Particular attention is given to behavioral intelligence, technique evolution, infrastructure reuse, credential targeting, campaign clustering, confidence assessment, and the limitations of indicator-only defense. The chapter establishes that threat intelligence becomes operationally useful only when it changes what defenders search for, what they prioritize, what they test, and which identity relationships they reduce before the adversary reaches them.
