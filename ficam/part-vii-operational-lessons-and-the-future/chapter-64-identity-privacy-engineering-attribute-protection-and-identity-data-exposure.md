# Chapter 64 - Identity Privacy Engineering, Attribute Protection, and Identity Data Exposure

### Abstract

Identity infrastructure concentrates information that can reveal who a person is, where they work, what systems they access, which devices they use, what privileges they possess, how they authenticate, and how they relate to mission systems and other personnel. In federal and DoD environments, identity security must therefore protect both authority and the sensitive information used to establish and administer that authority. This chapter examines privacy engineering across Active Directory, FICAM and ICAM repositories, PIV/CAC, biometrics, Microsoft Entra ID, PKI, federation, provisioning systems, applications, telemetry, identity-governance platforms, and attack-path data. Offensive analysis focuses on directory harvesting, identity correlation, attribute abuse, biometric compromise, privilege inference, bulk enumeration, telemetry exploitation, and data exfiltration. Defensive analysis emphasizes attribute minimization, access control, provenance, encryption, disclosure limitation, retention, pseudonymous identifiers, auditing, compartmentation, and privacy-aware detection engineering. The chapter establishes that identity data must be protected according to both the authority it enables and the harm its disclosure, manipulation, or correlation could create.
