# Chapter 66 - Identity Security in Operational Technology, Cyber-Physical Systems, and Mission Environments

### Abstract

Federal and DoD identity infrastructure increasingly intersects with operational technology, industrial control systems, cyber-physical platforms, tactical networks, building systems, logistics systems, laboratory environments, and mission systems whose compromise can produce physical or operational consequence. This chapter examines how Active Directory, PIV/CAC, Public Key Infrastructure, service accounts, local accounts, vendor identities, jump hosts, remote-access platforms, machine identities, certificates, and management infrastructure extend authority into environments where availability and safety requirements may prevent conventional enterprise security practices. Offensive analysis focuses on inherited domain trust, shared credentials, remote maintenance, credential reuse, engineering workstations, service identities, legacy authentication, vendor pathways, and administrative systems that bridge enterprise and operational networks. Defensive analysis emphasizes constrained identity boundaries, privileged-access isolation, non-person entity governance, certificate-based trust, controlled remote administration, segmentation, local survivability, high-fidelity monitoring, and recovery procedures designed around mission consequence. The chapter establishes that operational identity security must constrain authority without assuming uninterrupted connectivity, modern protocols, or the freedom to disrupt mission systems during testing or response.
