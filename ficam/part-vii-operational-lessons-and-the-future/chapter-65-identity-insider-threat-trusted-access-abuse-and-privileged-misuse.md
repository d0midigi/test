# Chapter 65 - Identity Insider Threat, Trusted Access Abuse, and Privileged Misuse

### Abstract

Identity-centric insider threat emerges when legitimate access, trusted position, administrative privilege, authorized credentials, or institutional knowledge is used in ways that exceed mission need or violate established trust. This chapter examines malicious insiders, negligent users, compromised trusted personnel, administrators, help-desk staff, developers, contractors, privileged operators, and other principals whose authorized access can become an attack path. Offensive analysis focuses on privilege accumulation, credential misuse, unauthorized delegation, policy manipulation, certificate abuse, cloud-role exploitation, data staging, telemetry suppression, persistence, and exploitation of trusted workflows. Defensive analysis emphasizes separation of duties, least privilege, privileged-access boundaries, behavioral and relationship analytics, high-risk transaction monitoring, immutable evidence, attack-path analysis, peer controls, rapid revocation, and post-incident trust reconstitution. Particular attention is given to distinguishing legitimate privileged activity from abuse without treating every anomalous action as malicious. The chapter establishes that insider defense is fundamentally an identity problem: trusted access must remain attributable, constrained, observable, revocable, and incapable of silently accumulating into unchecked authority.
