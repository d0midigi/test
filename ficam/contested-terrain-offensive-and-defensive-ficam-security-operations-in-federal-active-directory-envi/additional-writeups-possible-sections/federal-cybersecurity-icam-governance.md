## Federal Cybersecurity ICAM Governance

The chapter stops being primarily "here is how federal identity governance is organized," and becomes "here is the landscape of rules, decisions, records, and expectations that shapes your AD identity posture - and here is how to read that landscape from both sides." The defender-as-attacker mindset means:

Governance is presented as something that **creates,** **constrains,** **and sometimes obscures** the AD identity attack surface, not just something that produces compliance artifacts. Where a single-lens chapter would describe a governance requirement, the dual-lens chapter describes the requirement **and the gap it most often leaves between attestation and reality**, because that gap is where the attacker probes and the defender must be able to see. The reader is invited, explicitly, to read governance documents the way an attacker would: for assumptions, for scope boundaries, for places where process substitutes for technical rigor, for identities and priorities that governance authorized but did not technically harden.

The tone of this chapter is consistent with the book's overall purpose. It does not mean the chapter becomes an exploitation guide - the exploitation still belongs in the technical chapters. It means the governance chapter builds the mental map that makes those later chapters land.
