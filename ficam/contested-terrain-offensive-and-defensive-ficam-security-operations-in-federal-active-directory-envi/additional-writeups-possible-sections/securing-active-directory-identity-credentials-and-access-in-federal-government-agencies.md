# Securing Active Directory Identity, Credentials, and Access in Federal Government Agencies

### Abstract

Federal agencies handle a wealth of personal data and classified resources: addresses, social security numbers, as well as medical and financial information. To keep these sensitive assets as secure as possible, a strong identity management strategy must be planned and formulated; one whick includes the most effective technologies - and what better place than to start with than with FICAM. Otherwise known as Federal Identity, Credential, and Access Management, FICAM refers to the United States government's approach to Identity, Credential, and Access Management (ICAM). Its tools, governing policies, and systems guide agencies in providing the right person entity with the right resource at the right time.

### Introduction

There are numerous federal laws, policies, and standards that influence the design of federal government FICAM programs. One of these - the Office of Management and Budget's _OMB 19-17 Memorandum_, sets out the federal government's position on identity, credential, and access management and issues multiple strategic goals for agencies to work toward. These goals are intended to modernize digital operations, thereby improving security and better serving mission partners and the nation.

To achieve these objectives, defenders of federal agencies and military installations, branches, and combatant commands must implement FICAM solutions that they can manage with confidence. The U.S. General Services Administration (GSA) lists solution providers that help government entities to securely meet the FICAM roadmap outcomes.

In this chapter, we will explore what OMB 19-17 means for federal and military agencies. This chapter also explores how identity solutions providers, such as Okta and Amazon Web Services (AWS) for GovCloud FICAM help lead agencies to better identity compliance by fulfilling FICAM requirements and performing at full potential.

### Breaking Down the OMB 19-17 Memorandum

The OMB 19-17 Memorandum contains six total sections. The most prevalent parts of the six sections are briefly outlined below.

**I. Contextualize identity in the federal government**

This section offers a crucial definition of **identity**, describing it as the "unique representation of a person, device, or technological subject that interacts with a federal subject or resource (e.g., federal information, systems, and facilities).

Additionally, it provides two different contexts for identity:

| Federal enterprise identity                                                                                                     | Public identity                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identities that agencies manage to achieve business goals, such as employees, contractors, enterprise users, and their devices. | Identities that agencies do not directly manage but interact with to achieve their goals, such as users at partnering agencies. |

These are the "need-to-know" definitions of what identity is and how it occurs in federal government identity operations. The subsequent sections of OMB 19-17 outline the various mission statements for agencies.

### Where FICAM Meets FedRAMP

While not specifically discussed in OMB 19-17, it is essential to consider the Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program (FedRAMP). FedRAMP provides government agencies with a standardized approach to assessing cloud products and services - and defines its security requirements and processes that solutions providers must adhere to obtain approved government use; therefore, it is important that agencies look for FedRAMP authorization when choosing their identity solutions providers.

**II. Manage identities, credentials, and access in modern federal government identity security postures**

Advances in technology have made digital operations faster and more efficient, but these performance gains come with serious challenges - from severe cyber threats to breaches of personally identifiable information (PII). As technology evolves, so too do the methods used by nation-state attackers and advanced persistent threats (APT).

In response, the federal government is paying greater attention and focusing more tools and resources to risk management, namely the Risk Management Framework (RMF). This means adopting policies that improve privacy and security while helping to conduct operations and deliver services without friction.

To make these improvements, agencies must identify, credential, monitor, and manage all subjects and objects who access federally available resources.
