# Chapter 51 - Identity Operations in Contested, Degraded, Disconnected, and Intermittent Environments

### Abstract

Federal and DoD identity systems must continue supporting mission operations when connectivity, federation, cloud services, certificate validation, directory replication, centralized logging, or authoritative identity services become degraded or unavailable. This chapter examines identity security in contested, degraded, disconnected, and intermittent environments, including tactical and expeditionary networks, disconnected Active Directory sites, limited-bandwidth links, denied communications, mission-partner environments, and cloud-dependent services operating without reliable upstream connectivity. Offensive analysis focuses on how adversaries exploit stale identity state, delayed revocation, cached credentials, unavailable telemetry, emergency privileges, weakened authentication requirements, replication gaps, and recovery synchronization. Defensive analysis emphasizes controlled local authority, credential lifetime, resilient PIV/CAC validation, read-only domain controllers, protected caching, offline authorization, emergency-access governance, synchronization reconciliation, independent logging, and restoration of authoritative state. The chapter establishes that mission continuity cannot depend on abandoning identity assurance: degraded operations require intentionally engineered trust boundaries that remain defensible when centralized identity infrastructure cannot be reached.
