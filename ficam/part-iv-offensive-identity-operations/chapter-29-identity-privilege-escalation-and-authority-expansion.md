# Chapter 29 - Identity Privilege Escalation and Authority Expansion

### Abstract

Privilege escalation in identity environments is the process of converting existing access, credentials, permissions, sessions, delegated rights, or configuration authority into greater effective control. This chapter examines how adversaries move from ordinary or limited identities toward local, domain, forest, cloud, PKI, application, and Tier 0 authority through group relationships, discretionary access-control lists, ownership, delegated administration, service identities, Kerberos delegation, Group Policy, certificate services, management platforms, hybrid identity, and recovery systems. Offensive analysis focuses on selecting the least disruptive path that proves increased authority, validating prerequisites, avoiding unnecessary privilege chasing, and recognizing when indirect control already satisfies the operational objective. Defensive analysis emphasizes least privilege, administrative tiering, shadow-administrator discovery, privilege-boundary monitoring, remediation of high-leverage control edges, and attack-path recalculation after change. The chapter establishes that escalation is not defined by obtaining Domain Admin; it occurs whenever a principal gains materially greater ability to influence identities, systems, policies, credentials, trust, or mission operations.
