My personal belief is that the only way to push society and technology foreword is to not be apprehensive or afraid to tear things apart, tinker with various items and fully understand how they work. I like to surround myself with people who see the merit and value to this, yet bring different aptitudes to the table. The sharing of information from our efforts, both internally and with the world, is designed to help educate people on where problems arise, how they might have been avoided, and how to find them on their own.

But how do we get people to understand what they will need to protect? What is the clue to what next attack will be if it does not yet exist? Often it is an easy task if one takes an offensive research stance. Look for new problems yourself. In doing so, the researcher will invariably end up reverse engineering the object under scrutiny and see where the faults and stress lines are. These are the critical and most crucial areas that spend he time and effort buttressing against future inevitable attacks, and by having a thorough understanding of the object that is being analyzed, it is more readily apparent how and where it can be deployed securely, and how and where it cannot. This is, after all, one of the many reasons why we have War Games and War Colleges in the physical world - the worst-case scenario should never come as a surprise.

But all is not roses, and while there will always be the necessity for some continual remediation of existing systems that are concurrent to the forward-thinking, secure design, of and strategic implementation, there are those who remain afraid. In an attempt to do the right thing, individuals may, often times, go about it in strange and perplexing ways. There have been bills passed and laws put into place that attempt to hinder, obfuscate, or restrict the amount of dissemination and disassembling and reverse engineering individuals that engage in it. There have been numerous attempts to secure insecure network and application-level protocols and communications channels by passing laws that make it illegal to look at the vulnerable parts instead of addressing the actual protocol issue at hand. There even seems to be a belief in various law enforcing agencies that if a local area network is the equivalent to a local neighborhood, and the problem is that there are no locks on any of the doors to the houses, the solution is to deploy more policing officers on the beat.

As the generation that will either turn security into an enabling technology or allow it to persist as the obstacle that it is perceived as today, it is up to us, as offensive and defensive security professionals and practitioners to look at the existing dilemma through strategic lenses going forward. We do that ultimately by understanding how current attacks work, how attacker methodologies, security bypass strategies and methods, and adversarial Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs) are handled and deployed to endpoints. We do that by also understanding what certain cyberattacks might take advantage of, what tools and utilities are leveraged to conduct the attack, where they originate from for attribution, and when the next wave of attacks might hit by conducting deep adversarial intelligence gathering and studying of current attack trends and baselines, and where they might be aimed next. We create *Proof of Concept (PoC)* tools and code simulated attacks line-by-line to effectively demonstrate to not only ourselves, but to others to educate about just how specific and certain cyberattacks work and where they are most greatest and most weakest when targeting a security infrastructure. We do this by postulating and providing advice, insight, providing recommendations and suggestions on how these things could potentially be faced and appropriately addressed before, or after the fact that it is too late. We must conduct ourselves and our actions in a professional, ethical, lawful, and responsible manner lest we provide those individuals that remain afraid to understand these problems and that there are too many reasons that may prevent us from further or better understanding tis field and line of work.

My hopes for this book is that it becomes an enabling tool in educating and encouraging individuals to discover and think creatively about computer and network security. There are plenty of documents available yet they only seem to just tell people what to repair, but not that many explain in thorough detail the threat model(s) that are used or how to even find flaws on their own. The people who are forward-thinkers enable and educate the world to the mental shift to the new security model, and the literature that document how processes actually work, will be remembered for a very long time. Let there be many of those people and large tomes of such literature.
