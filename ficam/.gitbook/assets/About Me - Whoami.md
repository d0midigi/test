What I Do
I've been in the security world since 1996, long before Active Directory or Kerberos attacks were even a thing.

My career formally began nearly two decades ago. After earning a BS in Networking and Communications Management with a specialized focus on Telecommunications Management and Security, I stepped into my first official role as a network engineer supporting a flat Department of Defense (DoD) Novell NetWare and eDirectory environment.

There was no DHCP.
Every single IP address was manually assigned by me via a small yellow sticky.

My IPAM solution (at the time) was an Excel spreadsheet and a vanilla Access database tracking an entire Class C network and a partial Class B block for the DMZ network.

That era shaped how I think and view security in the world of information technology.

Since then, I've built experience across both public and private sectors, accumulating hard-earned lessons the only way they come: through responsibility and consequence.

Why I Believe the '90s Still Matter

The late '90s-early 2000s honor the era when I first learned how the AOL Instant Messenger (AIM) actually sent messages and how they traveled from Point A (source) to Point B (destination) and vice versa. The whole concept of not being able to logically understand how a piece of electronic mail made it to and from. Obviously, I had a thorough understanding of how the physical postal mailing system works, but because I couldn't see or hold the message yet it was there somehow in "thin air" making its way to an intended recipient just completely perplexed and boggled my mind. I had to find out how that worked. I remember also enduring the same confusion and curiosity the first time I learned about FTP as a freshman in college. OCE I "got it" the light clicked on for most everything else that came afterward came to me like second nature. I knew that the cyber world was for me. Since those days of early curiosity and the pursuit of knowledge has led me down a winding path where packets, protocols, and electrons moving through cables felt like peeking behind reality's curtain.

That curiosity never left.

A whole two decades later, I retain that curiosity I had as a young adult finding her way in the quagmire of the security realms.

Lessons Learned

I've failed. More times than I'd like to admit. A few made "there's no crying in baseball."

This field teaches resilience and bounce-back quickly. Thinking like an adversary, your opponent, changes your way of thinking permanently, not so much in a brain-washing way, but in a way that if you're dedicated to the profession you take in everything like a sponge. Becoming dedicated to the profession meant doing a lot of things i dint so much enjoy but then stuff i did enjoy and a lot of what you go through as an analyst trying to find her way around sometimes the only choice you have is to learn. If not and you fail, your heart was never truly in the right place. Constant methodologies and ways to do this and that sets you up with a mind frame that has security being slowly baked in. After a while it just becomes part of your persona. Everything you do in ordinary life, you run through the worst possible case scenarios or the what ifs. It sets up up to critically think on your toes, to have all of your what if checked to leave little to no rom for denial, to question everything because for every outcome there is a reason behind it, and to always think the worst (aka assume breach and to always have a backup plan on deck.). This field teaches resiliency quickly. Thinking like an adversary changes you permanently. Even away from dashboards and terminals, the mindset stays.

It taught me to think creatively and strategically.
To approach problems from angles others ignore.
To question assumptions under pressure.
To treat knowledge as leverage.

Cybersecurity made me a critical thinker. It also taught me to question everything, especially when it may be uncomfortable. It also taught me to respect the woman I became because of it.

I may still be a guppy in a vast ocean, but I can swim confidently among dolphins and sharks now.
