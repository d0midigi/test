# Chapter 52 - Cross-Domain, Coalition, and Mission-Partner Identity Operations

### Abstract

Federal and DoD identity security frequently extends beyond a single agency, command, classification level, tenant, forest, or national boundary. This chapter examines identity operations across mission-partner environments, coalition networks, cross-domain architectures, federated services, external directories, partner public key infrastructures, guest identities, foreign-national access, non-person entities, and shared mission applications. Offensive analysis focuses on how adversaries exploit transitive trust, inconsistent assurance, stale partner accounts, federation weaknesses, attribute manipulation, certificate trust, synchronization, shared administrative dependencies, and authorization decisions made from externally asserted identity. Defensive analysis emphasizes explicit trust boundaries, local authorization, partner-specific privilege, Identity Assurance Level, Authenticator Assurance Level, Federation Assurance Level, PIV/CAC and partner credential validation, certificate-policy enforcement, attribute provenance, continuous recertification, telemetry exchange, rapid revocation, and coordinated incident response. The chapter establishes that mission-partner identity must enable operational collaboration without allowing external trust to become uncontrolled internal authority.
