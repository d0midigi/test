# Discovering Your Target Network

Discovering Your Target Network

With the proliferation of the internet as a regular part of daily life for many, the effects of the level of information that can be accessed at moment’s notice is bewilderingly staggering. Only a few minutes, a topic, and a search engine are needed to access an incredible amount of information with just a few keystrokes and mouse clicks. As a result, it is becoming imperative to know what information regarding your target network is available on the internet. The aim of this section is to guide you as you discover what information is readily available concerning your network.

All that is really necessary to compromise most networks is time, patience, and most importantly – information. There are, however, several distinct steps that, if followed, can give the hacker greater chances at gaining initial access to their target network. The next step for the hacker is to identify key systems, and applications running on those systems. Finally, once a hacker has identified key systems and applications, they may continue on their path and produce a general internet search on each system looking for key vulnerabilities. Each of these steps will be covered in subsequence sections.

Now, to understand why gathering information on your target is one of the most important parts of attempting to illegally access a network. Most successful hackers will not attempt to break into a network on a whim; most successful hackers will attempt to learn as much about your network as possibly feasible. This is similar to how successful bank robberies are conducted. First, you familiarize yourself with the target, you learn its strengths and weaknesses, you look for ways to exploit those found weaknesses and then you go about committing your crime. The same steps are necessary when breaking into a network.

Below is a longer, more in-depth list of the steps that a hacker should take to gain information on their target:

1. Find initial information about the target
2. Find the IP address range of the network
3. Find active machines
4. Find active ports
5. Discover what operating systems each active machine is using
6. Discover which services each port is running
7. Search for vulnerabilities for each service

**Step 1: Find Initial Information About The Target Network**

Before anyone can even begin to scan your network for vulnerabilities, they have to familiarize themselves with the company’s _“lay of the land;”_ their network layout, or topology, so to speak. One way of familiarizing yourself with a network is to conduct a search for _**Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT).**_ An open-source search consists of gathering information that is readily available on the internet, or even around you. Open-source data and intelligence can be found on a company’s homepage, the newspaper, a trash can or dumpster, or anywhere else, really. The data provided by this search can yield a wealth of information that is useful when conducting a _**social engineering attack.**_

When an adversary starts their _**Reconnaissance**_ on a target company, system, or network, the first place of interest is the target’s parent website. While at this site, look for any information that may help you on your quest to enter the network. Key pieces of information you may try to search for are:

* Personnel directories
* New hardware, operating systems, security products
* The type of information they contain
* What they company’s most valuable asset is (e.g., what are it’s _“crown jewels?”_)
* How the target is organized (e.g., security guard shift changes, network traffic baselining (e.g., network behaviors – when does it spike? If so? Why? After baselining, you’ll come to the conclusion that the network spikes every other morning almost to the dot as those two days are when employees need to come into the office from remote work and they all login at the same time, thus causing the spike. Clearly from baselining any type of activity as such will yield patterns and from those patterns and trends, will give you better ideas to strategize for when the best time to attack would be. Easier to do it with more there online to pwn, or easier to do it at night when all have gone home and the network is _‘quiet.’_
* Key personnel and phone numbers

The first piece of information you should look for is a personnel directory or the name of key personnel. Using this information and a search engine such as Yandex, or The Wayback Machine, you can start to plan a social engineering attack.

In addition to searching for the personnel directory you should also look at the news releases sections. In this section, you are looking for information regarding any new products or changes to the company infrastructure that has been introduced. Frequently cutting edge companies will try to leverage new software that they are working with to attract new customers (e.g., _“All of our Servers now run Windows 2000 Advanced Server”_). Using this information a hacker can begin to narrow their vulnerability search to Windows Advanced server problems.

Using the data you learned from your searches you might have enough information to conduct a social engineering attack. For example, you may know that Sam and Ella work in the technical support division by using information from a personnel directory on the website. Reading the news section, you may also know that many members of the technical staff recently attended a SANS conference in San Diego, CA. Using that person’s name, the conference attended, location, and your trusty search engine of choice, what you may find may surprise you. You may be able to discover more than what you originally sought. While sometimes you may not learn anything of use or importance you may find that Sam and Ella attended a workshop on securing _**Internet Information Services (IIS)**_ servers. This information can lead you to believe that this company’s IIS servers may be vulnerable. Or you may use the information your learned though your searches to gain someone’s trust at the company, convince them that you are a friend of Sam and Ella, and that you need information on a server within their network or to even create an account for you.
