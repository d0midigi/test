# Ethical Qualities and Traits an Ethical Hacker Must Maintain

Ethical Qualities and Traits an Ethical Hacker Must Maintain

**Privacy**

As an ethical hacker, you will encounter information that you explicitly are **not allowed to use, steal, share, modify, change, or destroy.** From security blueprints, to account numbers to customer databases and intellectual property information, your access is limited. It is, however, your sole responsibility to safeguard that information at all times.

**Disclosure**

Ethical hackers need to have a tough skin when it comes to unearthing information you weren’t prepared for. It is not uncommon for ethical hackers to uncover things that are uncomfortable to see, watch, or talk about. If they stumble upon such information or content, their duty is to report it. Immediately. They owe it to the authorities or the concerned people to disclose evyerything they discover, however unsettling, gross, grave, or discomforting the content may be. You have been warned.

When an ethical hacker discovers an issue in software or hardware, they verify with or notify the hardware manufacturer that their product is faulty before going public with the information about the undisclosed vulnerability. If the manufacturer does nothing about it, they blow the whistle to save users and share the solution, if at all possible. Some folks would refer to this as a _Zero-Day_ vulnerability, meaning that the vulnerability has been discovered before the vendor had any idea it even existed.

**Area of Expertise**

An ethical hacker should not misinterpret themselves, feigning to know more than they do. Ethical hacking demands honesty and transparency about what an ethical hacker can do and cannot do, and openness about their level of knowledge, skill sets, and limitations. If you lack the necessary expertise or experience to handle something that is outside your realm of feasibility, it is ethical to ask the company or employer to get an expert to handle it.

**Unauthorized Usage**

An ethical hacker is to avoid using illegal or unethically (“pirated”) software and hardware. Also, if they uncover evidence of unauthorized usage in a company, they should not accept bribes to keep their lips sealed or join for personal gain.

**Authorization**

An ethical hacker needs to limit themselves to using resources, data, and/or information in authorized ways. Also, when working, an ethical hacker lets the company know how they intend to use data or information collected, or retrieved. They should also ensure that they get consent where necessary and avoid cutting corners.

**Project Management**

Ethical hackers need great project management skills to be efficient and to manage their projects effectively. They need to set clear and realistic goals, have a reasonable project timeline, and communicate.

**Knowledge and Information Sharing**

Ethical hackers commit to being a life-long learner, aiming to always keep abreast of new developments, sharing new discoveries, engaging fellow hackers, and creating public awareness. They do this by teaching or giving back to the cybersecurity communities, providing essential information through forums such as free lectures, spreading information on social media platforms, and enlighting the people they know on securing hardware and software and how to use this knowledge wisely.

**Confidence**

Confidence, as an ethical hacker, goes a long way, and means you should always present yourself in a professional, honest, and competent manner. This means even when you’re competing with someone else for a particular job, or project. In layman’s terms, no backstabbing, or blackmailing. Now, as we go through the chapters in this book, we’re going to be introducing some tools that can be extremely dangerous to networks. As an ethical hacker, you need to make sure that you have experience with any software, tricks, or tools you utilize against a network. An engagement is not the time or place to learn a new tool or technique. You need to be extremely careful. Do not fix issues you discover that are not within the scope of your assessment. Even if you think you know what’s best for your company or their company, you always get guidance and permission before you make any moves. There is no compromise. What I mean by this is that you are in no way going to purposely compromise or cause a company or organization’s system to become compromised through the process of your professional dealings with them.

**Legal Limits**

Whatever project an ethical hacker accepts needs to be approved, authorized, and legal. Period. The hacker code of ethics informs all their decisions. They always know what they are doing and what’s expected of them; they are aware of their limitations, know what they can and cannot do, and know what’s considered fair play and what’s malicious.

**Underground Communities**

While visiting underground communities and black hat-related forums is not uncommon in cybersecurity and ethical hacking, as cyber investigators and security researchers frequently visit the dark web and underground marketplaces to gather intelligence and information that’s not published on the indexed internet, ethical hackers commit to not engaging in illegal and unethical black hat hacking activities or engagements. They don’t aid or help black hat hackers advance their missions; they only engage them to find out what’s new, what they know, what they do, and how they think.

**Ethical Hacking**

Ethical hacking is a proactive cybersecurity approach that involves the use of hacking methods, concepts, and tools used to uncover weaknesses in systems or networks before malicious actors do.

An ethical hacker thinks like an malicious black hat hacker or a criminal profiler. They know how to steal passwords and usernames, and how to find and exploit vulnerabilities and get away with it. Ethical hackers use the same tactics that the bad guys use. The only difference is that they have permission to do so. Also, ethical hackers bring more to the table. Here are some of the most important knowledge, skills, abilities, ideal behaviors, and principles an ethical hacker uses:

* **Expertise in Architectures, Programs, and Networks:** You need to know the ins and outs of how the Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol (TCP/IP) works. You **must** understand computer network infrastructure and functions before you even begin to hack anything. If you don’t know networks, you won’t know hacking. You don’t necessarily need to be an expert in all programs, but you do need to have a general idea of how programs are installed, the modifications they can make, and the possible security threats they can pose.
* **Proficiency in Vulnerability Research:** Ethical hackers need to keep up with the bad guys at all paces. So, find out as much as you can about new vulnerabilities. Additionally, ensure that you are good at the hacking techniques that malicious hackers use because you might need to try them out on a particular target.
* **A Good Understanding of the Hacking Techniques Black Hat Hacker Use:** Ethical hackers are not vendor-specific; they understand the techniques bad actors use across the board – the hacks that can be used against routers, Intrusion Detection Systems (IDSs), and more.
* **Disclose to the Appropriate Parties:** Ethical hackers owe it to their employers and clients to disclose what they seek out to accomplish. They have a duty to disclose to authorities what they uncover if it is illegal. Ethical hackers also come across vulnerabilities in their client’s systems or infarstructures when evaluating them. The code bars them from exposing such information. They know what is off limits.
* **They Are Good at the Tactics and Strategies Black Hat Hackers Use:** They knowm the tactics and techniques and the strategies. They don’t just make stuff up or take shortcuts, or the easy way out.
* **They Understand That No Means No:** If a client or employer gives specific guidelines of what’s allowed and what’s not, am ethical hacker will draw a line between the two and respect that decision. They will try to cross it, amd if there is a change in scope, they ensure it is done in writing with applicable signatures of all parties involved and concerned with the operation to commence.
* **They Never Target a System or Network They Don’t Own or Are Not Allowed To:** It’s not a skill, per se, but if during a test a good ethical hacker is told to _stop_, they do so. Failure to do so has turned the lives of some ethical hackers upside down.
* **They Note and Report Meaningful and Actionable Results:** They report _all_ results, even if it might hurt someone whose good graces or books they want to be in, such as the manager who recommended them for that penetration testing job.
* **They Report Their Findings to Legal Authorities:** They know they have a duty to report illegal activities or plans to the authorities, and such, they ensure that the contract allows that; however, some jurisdictions place a legal responsibility on ethical hackers, even if the contract forbids such disclosures. Failure to report to legal authorities makes the ethical hacker an accessory to the crime. There are other ways out of such situations, including laws such as the Whistleblower Protection Act, which safeguards ethical hackers in specific types of disclosures.

* **Confidentiality:** When an ethical hacker encounters information that is deemed confidential, they know who to share their findings with, unless it goes against the laws of the land.
