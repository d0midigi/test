# Attack Methodology

Attack Methodology

For any attack to be successful, a set of rules, steps, or phases to conduct one are drawn out in what is known as a methodology. Not all methodologies need to be followed exactly in the order in which they are given, but it is extremely helpful if you try to hit the most important points important parts of the methodology to ensure the best outcome and result. There are attack stages to follow. For this attack scenario, I’ll follow this attack methodology:

1. Gathering Information: This involves collecting detailed data about our target, including the operating system, IP address, network resources, hardware type, firmware version, and topology. This step, known as reconnaissance or footprinting is crucial for us to understand the target’s environment.
2. **Scanning:** During this stage, we attempt to identify security holes in the operating system that can be exploited. This involves mapping the different services running on the system to find potential entry points.
3. **Identifying Vulnerabilities:** Here, we attempt to look for weaknesses or errors in the system, such as boundary condition errors, access validation errors, input validation errors, and failures to handle exceptional conditions. These vulnerabilities can be exploited one by one to compromise the system.
4. **Penetration:** In this final stage, we will carefully and gradually penetrate the system’s weak points. This involves continuously updating information from the security community to find new exploitation methods. The process of scanning and penetration can be carried out alternately to ensure thorough exploitation.
