# Appendix B Career and Certification Tracks

APPENDIX B

Career Tracks and Certification Paths

The goal of this appendix is to introduce the various certifications related to Cyber and Information Security and provide tips on building a successful career as an Ethical Hacker, Penetration Tester, Vulnerability Assessor in Cyber and Information Security.

According to a recent report from _Forbes,_ the cyber security market is expected to grow from $75B in 2024 to $170B by 2030. According to another survey, there are around 209,000 vacant cyber security jobs in the United States alone. This clearly indicates that there is a huge demand for cyber security professionals worldwide. The leaders of major economies are also expressing serious concerns over the cyber security threats to their nations. With this being said, this appendix introduces some of the most in-demand Cyber and In formation Security certifications and some add-on tips to take your professional career to the next level.

The Next Steps

In this appendix, we have seen a wide list of Cyber and Information Security certificates that you could opt for depending on your specific areas of interest; however, in addition to these certifications, there are a couple of items that need to be addressed beforehand to strengthen your skills and knowledge to weave into your professional career and certification paths.

Learning Programming Languages

In the field of Cyber and Information Security, it is most essential to know at least a few of the common programming languages. This gives you the power to automate tasks and to streamline to perform them more efficiently. A few of the programming languages worth understanding are:

* C
* PHP
* Python
* JavaScript
* SQL Basics
* Macros using VB (VisualBasic)
* Basics of Assembly Language
* Shell Scripting
* PowerShell

The following sections feature tips on how to improve your professional profile.

Bug Bounty

Bug Bounty is a program where organizations acknowledge or pay renumeration to whoever finds security bugs, exploits, and flaws within their applications. Finding a security bug in a website or mobile application and reporting it to the concerned organization gives you a great amount of confidence in your abilities and skills. It also readily demonstrates your skills to prospective employers.

Social Presence

Social networking platforms can be effectively utilized to network with the cyber communities and increase visibility. LinkedIn can be used to build a profile snapshot and connect with other professional peers. Substack can be used to create a page or community and share your thoughts on topics of relatable interests.

Publishing Articles and Research Papers in Forms of Digital Publications

Many local and international magazines, online forums, blogs, journals, and websites publish articles on various topics of Cyber and Information Security. There’s a distinguished advantage in presenting and publishing articles in such media. Your work reaches a larger audience across the world. A few such forums are:

* _Pentest Magazine_
* _Hackin9_
* _Hackinsight_
* _SC Magazine_

Developing Tools

There are many readily available commercial and open-source tools automating various security-related tasks; however, there might be many problem areas for which there are no tools available. Such areas should be identified, studied, and researched thoroughly. A tool could be developed to solve a particular problem related to Cyber and Information Security. Modern programming languages like Python make it easy for anyone to develop custom toolsets. Once the tool is developed, it can be shared through public communities like GitHub. Contributing something new to the cyber communities adds substantial value to your profile.
