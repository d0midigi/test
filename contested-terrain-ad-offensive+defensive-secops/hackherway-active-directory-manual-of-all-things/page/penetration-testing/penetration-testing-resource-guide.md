If you are interested in getting an industry standard security certification, there is a wide array of network-related and ethical hacking, offensive security, penetration testing, and professional certifications for you to choose from. The list below contains some of the most-popular, and most in-demand certifications from professionals as of this writing in 2024.

* **Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH):** Issued by the EC-Council, the CEH is a good “starter” certification, so it’s a perfect option if you’re just getting your feet wet in security.
* **Offensive Security Certified Professional (OSCP):** Offered by the Offensive Security organization, this certification is easily the most well-known and is highly sought after. The OSCP exam is fairly difficult for anyone who is just starting out in security, but the good news is that it doesn’t require renewal.
* **Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP):** This is a certification you’ll want to get if you aspire to be a security leader. It is intended to teach how to effectively design, implement, and manage a best-in-class cybersecurity program. It is geared more for those in mid-level, or senior managerial positions.
* **GIAC Penetration Tester (GPEN):** SANS GIAC certifications are some of the world’s most reputable names in security training. The GPEN certification focuses on technical deep dives of many facets of web, network, application, physical, information, and cyber securities.

A mentor can help you dig into technical subject matter that you want to familiarize yourself with (e.g., enhancing your network pentesting skills), as well as soft skills to develop effective communications and management. Other skills mentors can help you improve include salary negotiation, interviewing, and advocating for yourself in the workplace. These skills might not be as flashy as red teaming, but they are still a very important and extremely beneficial trait to have in your professional development.

How Do You Get Started?

* Begin with your network (co-workers, professors, or teachers, friends, classmates, and even family members)
* Look up to someone at work or online. Reach out and talk to people!
* Network, network, network: Take full advantage of casual online or in-person meetups and local conferences.
* Try the weekly _#CyberMentoringMonday_ thread via Tanya Janca (@SheHacksPurple).

CAPTURE THE FLAG (CTF) EVENTS

CTFs are a gamified exercise designed to test cybersecurity skills, with the goal, much like in the live-action, outdoor game, of obtaining the highest score by capturing the most flags.

There are hundreds of CTF events taking place every year, which means ample opportunity to find some that fit your timing and needs just right. Some CTFs are virtual, while others happen on-site at a conference or meeting. A few I recommend:

* **PicoCTF:** Perfect for young minds in STEM who want to take their coding hobby to the next level, PicoCTF provides year-round cybersecurity education content (PicoGym practice challenges) for learners of all skill levels.

_(**Source:** picoctf.org)_

* **Red Team Village:** Offers a couple of events per year, plus Red Team stations where attendees can practice their skills and interactive workshops focused on web attack training, HackerOps, hacker APIs, OSINT skills lab, and more.

_(**Source:** redteamvillage.io)_

* **DEF CON:** One of the most elite competitions available to hackers. The teams conduct reverse engineering, pwning, and pushing other hackers off their boxes in the head-to-head competition to directly demonstrate effective exploitation for the future.

_(**Source:** forum.defcon.org)_
