# Glossary1

### Glossary

### APT (Advanced Persistent Threat)

A prolonged and targeted cyberattack in which an intruder gains access to a network and remains undetected for an extended period.

### Air Gap

A security measure that involves isolating a computer or network from any outside connections.

### Attack Vector

The method or pathway used by a hacker to breach a system.

### Backdoor

A hidden way to bypass normal authentication and gain access to a system.

### Backscatter

Unsolicited emails sent in response to spam with forged sender addresses.

### Black Hat Hacker

A hacker who exploits vulnerabilities for malicious purposes.

### Blacklist

A list of entities (e.g., IP addresses, email addresses) that are blocked because they are considered harmful.

### Blue Team

A group of security professionals responsible for defending an organization against attacks and improving security measures.

### Blue Teaming

Defensive measures taken by an organization to protect against and respond to attacks.

### Bot

An automated program that performs tasks over the internet, often used in botnets for malicious activities.

### Bot Herder

The individual who controls a botnet.

### Botnet

A network of infected devices controlled by a hacker to perform coordinated attacks.

### Bricking

Rendering a device completely unusable.

### Browser Hijacking

Manipulating a web browser to redirect traffic to malicious sites.

### Brute-Force Attack

An attack that tries all possible combinations of passwords to gain access.

### Bug Bounty

A program where organizations offer rewards to individuals who find and report security vulnerabilities.

### Bug

An error or flaw in software that can be exploited by hackers.

### Buffer Overflow

An attack that occurs when more data is written to a buffer than it can hold, causing data to overwrite adjacent memory.

### Cache Poisoning

An attack that corrupts DNS cache data to redirect traffic.

### Catfishing

Creating a fake online identity to deceive others.

### Chaffing

A technique used to disguise real data by mixing it with random data.

### Click Fraud

Illegally clicking on online ads to generate revenue for the attacker.

### Clickjacking

A malicious technique tricking users into clicking on something different from what they perceive, potentially revealing confidential information or taking control of their computer.

### Command and Control (C\&C or C2)

A server that sends commands to infected devices (bots) in a botnet.

### Cracker

A hacker with malicious intent, often referred to as a black hat hacker.

### Credential Harvesting

Collecting usernames and passwords, often through phishing attacks or compromised databases.

### Credential Stuffing

Using stolen username-password pairs from one breach to attempt unauthorized access to other systems.

### Cryptojacking

Unauthorized use of someone else's computing resources to mine cryptocurrency.

### Cyber Terrorist

A hacker motivated by political or religious beliefs, targeting critical infrastructure to cause fear or disruption.

### Data Breach

An incident where sensitive, protected, or confidential data is accessed, disclosed, or stolen.

### Data Exfiltration

Unauthorized transfer of data from a system.

### Denial of Service (DoS)

An attack that aims to make a system or network resource unavailable to its intended users.

### Deep Packet Inspection (DPI)

Analyzing the data part (and possibly also the header) of a packet as it passes an inspection point.

### Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)

An attack that uses multiple systems to overwhelm a target with traffic, causing a service outage.

### Domain Name System (DNS) Spoofing

Manipulating DNS records to redirect traffic from a legitimate site to a malicious one.

### Drive-by Download

Unintentional download of malware to a computer or mobile device without the user's consent.

### Dropper

Malware is designed to install other malware onto a system.

### Dumpster Diving

Searching through physical trash to find sensitive information that can be used in an attack.

### Eavesdropping Attack

Intercepting and listening to private communications.

### Encryption

The process of converting data into a coded format to prevent unauthorized access.

### Evil Twin Attack

Creating a fake Wi-Fi network that appears legitimate to trick users into connecting.

### Exploit

A piece of code or software that takes advantage of a vulnerability to execute malicious actions.

### Firewall

A network security device that monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic.

### Flood Attack

Overloading a network or service with excessive data to cause disruption.

### Footprinting

The process of gathering information about a target system or network.

### Formjacking

Injecting malicious code into online forms to steal payment card details and other sensitive information.

### Gray Hat Hacker

A hacker who may violate laws or ethical standards but does not have malicious intent.

### Hacker

A person who uses their technical skills to gain unauthorized access to systems or networks.

### Hacktivist

A hacker who uses their skills for political or social activism.

### Heuristic Analysis

A method used by antivirus programs to detect new, unknown malware based on behavior patterns.

### Honeypot

A decoy system designed to attract and trap attackers.

### Honeytoken

A fake digital asset used to detect unauthorized access or theft.

### Insider Threat

A security risk that originates from within the organization, typically involving employees or contractors.

### Intrusion Detection System (IDS)

A system that monitors network traffic for suspicious activity and issues alerts.

### Intrusion Prevention System (IPS)

A system that detects and prevents identified threats in real-time.

### Jailbreaking

Removing restrictions on a device to allow for unauthorized modifications and access.

### Juice Jacking

Installing malware on a device or stealing data from it through a compromised USB charging station.

### Key Exchange

The process of sharing encryption keys between parties to establish a secure communication channel.

### Keylogger

Software or hardware that records keystrokes to capture sensitive information such as passwords.

### Logic Bomb

Malicious code that triggers a harmful action when certain conditions are met.

### Lone Wolf

A lone wolf hacker operates independently, without affiliation to any hacking group, organization, or state. They work alone, driven by personal motives which can range from curiosity and challenge to revenge or financial gain. These hackers typically rely on their own skills and resources to carry out attacks.

### Malware

Malicious software is designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to systems.

### Man-in-the-Browser (MitB) Attack

Malware that intercepts and modifies web transactions in real-time.

### Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) Attack

An attack where the hacker intercepts and alters communication between two parties.

### Packet Sniffing

Capturing and analyzing data packets traveling over a network.

### Patch

A software update that fixes vulnerabilities or bugs.

### Payload

The part of malware that performs malicious action, such as data theft or system damage.

### Penetration Testing

A method used by white hat hackers to test the security of a system by attempting to exploit vulnerabilities.

### Phishing

A form of social engineering where attackers trick individuals into providing sensitive information, often via email.

### Phreaking

Hacking into telecommunication systems.

### Piggybacking

Similar to tailgating but involves gaining entry with the knowledge and consent of the authorized person.

### Pivoting

Using a compromised system to attack other systems within the same network.

### Polymorphic Malware

Malware that constantly changes its code to avoid detection by antivirus software.

### Public Key Infrastructure (PKI)

A framework for managing digital certificates and public-key encryption.

### Rainbow Table

Precomputed table for reversing cryptographic hash functions, used in cracking password hashes.

### Ransomware

Malware that encrypts a victim's data and demands payment for the decryption key.

### RAT (Remote Access Trojan)

Malware that allows an attacker to take control of a victim's system remotely.

### Reconnaissance

The initial phase of hacking is where the attacker gathers information about the target.

### Red Team

A group of security professionals who simulate real-world attacks to test an organization's defenses.

### Red Teaming

A simulated attack on an organization to test its defenses and response strategies.

### Root Access

The highest level of access to a system, providing full control.

### Rootkit

Software designed to enable continued privileged access to a computer while actively hiding its presence.

### Sandbox

A security mechanism for separating running programs to prevent malware from affecting the system.

### Script Kiddie

An inexperienced hacker who uses pre-written scripts or tools to conduct attacks.

### Security Breach

An incident where unauthorized access to data, applications, services, networks, or devices is achieved.

### Security Information and Event Management (SIEM)

A system that aggregates and analyzes security data from various sources in real-time.

### Shoulder Surfing

Observing someone's private information by watching their screen or keyboard.

### Skimming

Stealing credit card information using a small device.

### Smishing

Phishing conducted via SMS text messages.

### Sniffing

Intercepting and logging traffic passing over a digital network.

### Social Engineering

Manipulating people into divulging confidential information.

### Spear Phishing

A targeted phishing attack aimed at a specific individual or organization.

### Spoofing

Masquerading as a trusted entity to deceive others.

### Spyware

Software that gathers information about a person or organization without their knowledge.

### SQLi (SQL Injection)

Inserting malicious SQL queries into input fields to manipulate a database.

### State-Sponsored Hacker

A hacker employed by a government to conduct cyber espionage or cyber warfare.

### Steganography

Concealing messages or information within other non-secret text or data.

### Suicide Hacker

A suicide hacker is an attacker who does not care about the consequences of their actions, including getting caught or facing legal repercussions. They are willing to execute high-risk attacks, often with the intention of causing maximum damage or disruption, without concern for their own safety or anonymity.

### Tailgating

Gaining physical access to a secure area by following closely behind someone with authorized access.

### Threat Intelligence

Information about current and emerging threats is used to inform security decisions.

### Traffic Analysis

Analyzing patterns in communication to infer information.

### Trojan Horse

Malware disguised as legitimate software.

### Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

An authentication method that requires two forms of verification.

### Two-Man Rule

A security protocol requiring two individuals to perform certain critical actions to prevent unauthorized activities.

### Typosquatting

Registering domain names that are similar to legitimate ones to trick users into visiting malicious sites.

### Virtual Private Network (VPN)

A secure connection over the Internet that provides privacy and anonymity.

### Virus

Malicious code that replicates itself and spreads to other systems.

### Warchalking

Drawing symbols in public places to indicate the presence and status of WiFi networks.

### Wardriving

Searching for Wi-Fi networks by driving around with a laptop or smartphone.

### Watering Hole Attack

Compromising a specific group of users by infecting websites they are known to visit.

### Whaling

A phishing attack targeting high-profile individuals.

### Whitebox Testing

Testing software with knowledge of its internal workings.

### White Hat Hacker

A hacker who uses their skills to improve security by finding and fixing vulnerabilities.

### Worm

A self-replicating malware that spreads across networks without user intervention.

### XSS (Cross-Site Scripting)

Injecting malicious scripts into web pages to attack users.

### Zeroday (Zero-Day Vulnerability)

A vulnerability that is unknown to the software vendor and for which no patch is available.

### Zombie

A computer controlled by a hacker, often part of a botnet.
