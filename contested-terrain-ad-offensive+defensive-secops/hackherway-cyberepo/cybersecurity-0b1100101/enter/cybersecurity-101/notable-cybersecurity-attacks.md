# Notable Cybersecurity Attacks

**Significant Cybersecurity Incidents**

Each year introduces a new wave of cyberattacks, reported either voluntarily by victims adhering to stringent regulations or unearthed by vigilant security analysts. The noteworthiness of these attacks often stems from their impact on high-profile entities or the scale of affected individuals. Here are some of the standout cyber incidents from the past decade:

**Adobe (2013):** In October 2013, Adobe disclosed a massive breach of its IT infrastructure, compromising personal information from 2.9 million accounts. Exploiting a security breach related to password practices, hackers gained access to logins, passwords, names, and credit card details.

**Adult Friend Finder (2015 and 2016):** A dating site known as Adult Friend Finder faced two significant breaches in 2015 and 2016. The first exposed information from 4 million accounts, while the second, more severe attack exposed over 400 million files, including 20 years' worth of personal information.

**Equifax (2017):** Equifax, a prominent credit company, revealed a cyberattack in 2017 that impacted 143 million American, Canadian, and British customers. Personal data such as names, birthdates, social security numbers, and credit card information were compromised.

**ILOVEYOU Virus (2000):** The ILOVEYOU virus, a notorious computer worm, infected over ten million personal computers worldwide in May 2000. It spread through email with the subject line "ILOVEYOU" and wreaked havoc by encrypting files.

**Marriott (2014):** A breach at the Marriott-owned Starwood hotel group began in 2014, compromising personally identifiable information from up to 500 million guests. The stolen data included payment information, names, addresses, passport numbers, and details about the Starwood Preferred Guest account.

**NotPetya Malware (2016):** Petya, a ransomware class, evolved into NotPetya in 2016, targeting Microsoft Windows-based systems. The malware demanded Bitcoin payments to regain access, spreading globally in a cyberattack on Ukraine.

**Sony (2011 and 2014):** Sony faced cyberattacks on multiple fronts. In 2011, the PlayStation Network (PSN) was compromised, leaking the personal data of 77 million users. In 2014, Sony Pictures Entertainment suffered a significant breach, with 100 terabytes of data, including film scripts and personal information, stolen.

**Target (2013):** In December 2013, Target experienced a massive cyberattack, compromising bank card records of 40 million customers and personal data of another 70 million customers.

**TJ Maxx (2007):** In 2007, TJX Companies, the parent company of TJ Maxx, reported a network intrusion resulting in the loss of sensitive data.

**WannaCry Ransomware Attack (2017):** The WannaCry ransomware attack in May 2017 targeted devices running Microsoft Windows, encrypting data and demanding ransom payments. It utilized the EternalBlue exploit, stolen from the NSA.

**Yahoo (2014):** In 2014, Yahoo disclosed a cyberattack affecting 500 million user accounts, leading to the theft of names, dates of birth, telephone numbers, and passwords. Subsequent estimates suggest up to 3 billion user accounts may have been impacted.

These incidents highlight the persistent challenges in securing digital information, necessitating continuous advancements in cybersecurity measures.
