# Cybersecurity Terminology, Slang, Lingo, 1337 Speak (LeetSpeak)

The imperative to safeguard personal information, intellectual property, and critical data intensifies with our reliance on technology. Motivated by various factors such as politics, social activism, or greed, threat actors operate globally, intercepting, exfiltrating, or disrupting data flows. Modern conflicts extend into cyberspace, emphasizing the crucial role of cybersecurity.

The escalating threats to data systems and breaches of sensitive information underscore the pressing need for qualified individuals capable of implementing robust security controls. Recognizing this demand, there is a call for technically adept individuals to join the cybersecurity profession.

This guide aims to introduce readers to the field of cybersecurity. It covers key industry terms, essential security principles, and offers a brief historical overview. For those contemplating a career in cybersecurity, this provides a comprehensive overview of what to expect.

**Understanding Cybersecurity: Terminology, Principles, and Historical Context**

**Foundational Vocabulary:** In delving into the expansive realm of cybersecurity or any technical discipline, mastering specialized terminology is imperative. These terms often possess meanings related to their general, non-technical use, but nuances specific to the field are crucial for comprehension. A lack of clarity regarding how security professionals employ these terms can lead to considerable confusion in the learning process.

Many security terms have origins so obscure that accurate usage requires an understanding of their historical context. For instance, the term "pwn" or "pwned," commonly used by hackers, likely stems from a mistyping of "own" due to the proximity of the letters P and O on a keyboard. When a hacker claims to have "pwned" a person or organization, they assert dominance or conquest.

**Defining Key Concepts:** The subsequent definitions elucidate common terms, abbreviations, and acronyms within the security domain.

* **Attack Surface:** The sum of various locations, logical or physical, where a threat actor can attempt to enter or extract data from a software-based system. Reducing the attack surface is a primary security measure.
* **AV (Antivirus):** Security software that scans for, detects, blocks, and eliminates malware. AV programs operate in the background, scanning for known malware signatures and behavior patterns.
* **Brute-force Attack:** An attempt to decipher a username-password combination through trial and error, employing software designed to try large samples of known combinations. Despite being an old method, it remains popular among hackers.
* **Cryptoworm:** A form of malware that spreads like a worm and encrypts victims' data.
* **Data Breach:** A security event where unauthorized users steal sensitive information from an organization's IT systems. Stolen data often includes personally identifiable information (PII) or financial information, both valuable on the dark web.
* **EDR (Endpoint Detection and Response):** A security tool focusing on detecting and mitigating suspicious activity on devices and hosts. EDR is valuable for identifying advanced threats lacking recorded behavioral patterns or malware signatures.

### Security Terminology Explained

* **Firewall:** A firewall is a network security system that monitors and controls network traffic based on specific rules. It establishes a barrier between a trusted internal network and an untrusted external network, like the Internet.
* **Honeypot:** A honeypot is software designed to detect, deflect, and counteract unauthorized attempts on information systems. It appears as legitimate data but is isolated and monitored, blocking attackers who interact with it.
* **IPS (Intrusion Prevention System):** An IPS is a network security form that detects and prevents identified threats. Continuously monitoring a network, it captures and reports information about potential malicious incidents.
* **Malware:** Malware is malicious software that spreads via email attachments or links to malicious websites, infecting endpoints when users interact with them.
* **NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology):** NIST, under the U.S. Department of Commerce, provides information security measures and controls through its Publication Series 800, based on extensive research.
* **Phishing/Spearphishing:** Phishing involves malicious emails tricking users into surrendering credentials. Spearphishing targets specific individuals with individually-crafted emails, often masquerading as legitimate requests.
* **Ransomware:** Ransomware, a malicious software class, denies access to systems or data. Crypto-ransomware encrypts files, demanding payment for decryption. Locker ransomware locks access, also demanding payment.
* **Ransomware Attack:** In ransomware campaigns, hackers use phishing and social engineering to trick users. Some attacks exploit vulnerabilities, infecting systems and demanding a ransom.
* **Risk Management Framework:** A disciplined process integrating information security and risk management into the system development life cycle. Components include identification, measurement, assessment, mitigation, reporting, monitoring, and governance.
* **Security Misconfigurations:** Resulting from improper security control implementation, misconfigurations on devices, networks, and systems can lead to breaches, unauthorized access, and security incidents. Examples include default admin credentials, open ports, unpatched software, unused web pages, and unprotected files.

### Key Concepts in Cybersecurity

* **SOC (Security Operations Center):** A Security Operations Center (SOC) serves as a central hub where cybersecurity professionals execute threat detection and incident response processes. The SOC utilizes various security technologies integral to an organization's security operations.
* **SQL Injection (SQLi):** SQL injection is a technique inserting structured query language (SQL) code into a web application database. Attackers exploit this to manipulate database data, spoof user identity, and execute remote commands, compromising web application security.
* **Tor (The Onion Router):** Tor, or The Onion Router, is open-source software facilitating anonymous communication. Routing Internet traffic through a global network of over seven thousand relays, Tor conceals user location and usage, thwarting network surveillance and traffic analysis.
* **VA (Vulnerability Assessment):** Vulnerability assessment involves identifying, classifying, and prioritizing vulnerabilities in digital business systems. Assessments may focus on internal, external, or host-based vulnerabilities.
* **VM (Vulnerability Management):** Vulnerability management solutions identify, track, and prioritize both internal and external cybersecurity vulnerabilities. These solutions streamline cyberattack prevention activities, including patching, upgrades, and configuration fixes.
