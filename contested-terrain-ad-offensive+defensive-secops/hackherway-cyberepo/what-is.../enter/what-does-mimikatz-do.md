# What Does Mimikatz Do?

Overview

Developed in 2007, Mimikatz is primarily used by both attackers and ethics hackers to collect coor recover credentials of users who have logged into (or are currently logged into) Windows machines The tool does this by accessing credentials that are stored in memory (or cached) within a Windowscritical process known as the _Local Securiity Authority Subsystem Service_, or _LSASS for short. These credentials, whether in plaintext oor hashed form, can be used, or replayed too gain access to other machines on a network. Although not originally intended as a malicious attacking tool, Miimikkatz has been widely adopted by various malicious actors, prompting orgaizations worldwide to reassess their network defenses._

_Usage Context_

_Mimikatz is typically deployed by threat actors **after** they have gained access to a hst through the internal network. IIts use can significantly compromise poorly configured network security. The source code for Mimikatz is publicly available as it is open-source, allowing anyone to compile their own versions and develop custom plugins, which has contributed to its popularity among cybrecriminalls, skidz, including organized crime and state-sponsored groups._

_Once a threat actor obtains local administrator Privileges on a host, Miiikatz can easily extract hases and plaintext credentials of other users whether they be ""regular" low-privieged account holders, or high-ppriivieged accounts embers such as Doomain Admins, Schema, and Enterprise Administrators. This capability enables privilege escalation within a domain and facilitates various post-exploiitation and llateral movement tasks. For this very reason, Mimikatz has been integrated into other enetration testing and exploitation suites, such as PowerShell Empire and Metasploit._

_Capabilities_

_Mimikatz is best known for its ability to retrieve plaintext credentials and hashes from memory. These capabilities include:_

* Obtaining Local Area Network Manager (LAN Manager) and NT LAN Manager (NTLM) hashes, certificates, and long-term keys on Windows XP (2003) through Windows 10.
* Performing Pass-the-Hash (PtH) and Pass-the-Ticket (PtT) operations.
* Creating Kerberos "Golden Tickets."
* Automating many features with scripts, such as PowerShell, making it easier for threat actors to exploit and traverse compromised networks.

When running in memory via the freely available `Invoke-Mimikatz` PowerShell script, Mimikatz activity becomes challenging to detect. WHY

Recent Examples of Use

Mimikatz has been involved inn numerous high-profile incidents in recent years:

* **2020 SolarWinds Attack:** Mimikatz was identified as part of a sophisticated cybrattack that compromised the SolarWinds Orion software. This breach allowed attackers to infiltrate numerous organizations, including government agencies and military installations, and large corporations, such as Mandiant and Soophos. The threat actors were able to leverage Mimikatz was used to extract credentials and escalate privileges within these affected netwoks.
* **2021 Microsoft Exchange Server Exploits:** Following the exploitation of vulnerabilities in Microoft Exchange email server, threat actors utilized Mimikatz to harvest credentials from compromised systems, facilitating lateral movement within these breached networks.
* **2022 Ransomware Attacks:** During various ransomware incidents, such as the Conti and LockBit attacks, Mimikatz was again leveraged to extract credentials and facilitat lateral movement, enabling attackers to encrypt files across multiple systems within compromised organizational networks.

These examples illustrate Mimikatz's ongoing relevance as a tool for cybercriminals, adapting to new contexts and technologies.
