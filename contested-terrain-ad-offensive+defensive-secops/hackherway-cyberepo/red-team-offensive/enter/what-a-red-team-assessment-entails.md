# What a Red Team Assessment Entails

<figure><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/red_team_shield_with_heart_2-removebg-preview.png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

The Red Team is the offensive side of cyber security - the ones who take on the role of adversaries to test and challenge an organization's defenses. In contrast to a standard penetration test, a Red Team Assessment is more targeted, with a primary focus on testing an organization's detection and response capabilities rather than simply uncovering vulnerabilities.

The goal of a Red Team Assessment is to emulate the tactics of a malicious actor, such as an Advanced Persistent Threat (APT), by infiltrating the target environment and accessing sensitive information without being detected. This covert approach aims to expose any weaknesses in the organization's security posture and response mechanisms, highlighting areas for improvement and fortification.

Unlike a penetration test, which typically lasts 1-2 weeks, a Red Team Assessment is a more extensive undertaking that can span 3-4 weeks or even longer. This extended duration allows the red team to conduct thorough and comprehensive assessments, often involving multiple team members working collaboratively to achieve their objectives.

Rather than focusing on quantity, a Red Team Assessment hones in on specific vulnerabilities that pose the greatest threat to the organization's security. By leveraging tactics like social engineering, both physical and electronic, wireless exploits, and external attacks, the red team simulates real-world threats to push the organization's defenses to their limits.

It's important to note that Red Team Assessments are not for every organization. They are best suited for entities with mature security programs that have already undergone penetration tests, patched vulnerabilities, and received positive test results. These organizations recognize the value of proactive testing and continuous improvement to stay ahead of evolving threats in the cyber landscape.
