# Default Accounts

Adversaries may obtain and exploit credentials of a default account to achieve Initial Access, Persistence, Privilege Escalation, or Defense Evasion. Default accounts, integral to an operating system (OS), include examples like the Guest or Administrator accounts on Windows systems, or default accounts on other types of systems, software, or devices, often factory or provider-set.

Default accounts extend beyond client machines to include those preset for equipment like network devices and computer applications, whether internal, open source, or commercial. Preset credentials in appliances, if unchanged post-installation, present a significant threat, making them susceptible targets for adversaries. Additionally, adversaries may leverage publicly disclosed or stolen Private Keys or credential materials to legitimately connect to remote environments through Remote Services.
